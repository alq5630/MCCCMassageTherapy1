Thai Massage Practicum

Combine rhythmic compression with assisted movement and stretching while protecting the client's joints and your own body mechanics.

Traditional format

Thai massage is commonly performed on a floor mat with the client clothed. Traditional systems may organize treatment around sen lines and other Thai medical concepts. This classroom practicum focuses on safe introductory bodywork skills rather than claiming mastery of the full tradition.

Practical sequence

  1. Establish comfortable positioning and explain that the client will be moved and stretched.
  2. Begin with broad rhythmic compression.
  3. Move joints slowly through available range.
  4. Use assisted stretches that are stable, controlled and easy for the client to stop.
  5. Coordinate your body weight rather than muscling the client through range.
  6. Transition smoothly between positions and reassess comfort.

Don't force flexibility

Thai massage can look dramatic, but the goal isn't maximum range. Modify or avoid stretches for hypermobility, acute injury, unstable joints, osteoporosis/fracture risk, neurological symptoms, recent surgery, pregnancy-related positioning needs, or any presentation where leverage could create harm.
