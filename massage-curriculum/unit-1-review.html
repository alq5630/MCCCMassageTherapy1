MBLEx reasoning pattern

For a scenario, ask in this order: Is massage appropriate at all? Is this area appropriate? Does the client need a modification? What technique best fits the goal? What response would make me change or stop? Safety and scope outrank showing off a technique.

Rapid knowledge check

A client reports a new hot, red, swollen calf with no known cause. They ask you to “work it out.” What's the best response?

Don't massage the symptomatic area. A new hot, red, swollen calf can indicate a condition requiring medical evaluation. The safest answer is referral rather than deep local work.

A client has a small healing abrasion on the forearm but otherwise feels well. Is the entire massage automatically contraindicated?

No. Avoid or appropriately protect the local area according to infection-control needs and work elsewhere if massage is otherwise appropriate.

Which stance is generally useful for long gliding strokes along the length of the table?

Archer or bow stance because it allows weight transfer from one leg to the other.

A client says the pressure is painful but tells you to continue because “deep tissue is supposed to hurt.” What should you do?

Reduce or modify the pressure. Pain isn't required for an effective massage and client feedback should guide treatment.

What are the three transmission categories emphasized in the course?

Contact, vehicle, and vector transmission.

How long should handwashing with soap and water last?

At least 20 seconds of scrubbing.

What's the difference between an absolute and local contraindication?

An absolute contraindication means massage is postponed entirely. A local contraindication means the affected area is avoided or modified while massage elsewhere may still be appropriate.

A therapist wants deeper pressure but notices their wrist bending sharply and shoulder elevating. What's the best correction?

Change stance, table position or contact tool so pressure comes from aligned body-weight transfer rather than muscular strain.

What does E-P-F-C-T-V stand for?

Effleurage, petrissage, friction, compression, tapotement, vibration.

Which technique family is rhythmic percussion?

Tapotement.

What is excursion?

The distance traveled by a massage technique.

What's the difference between rate and rhythm?

Rate is speed or repetition frequency. Rhythm is the regular pattern and predictability of movement.
