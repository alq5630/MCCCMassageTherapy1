Unit 2 · Practical

Neck & Chest

Complete the foundational supine routine with supported neck work and carefully bounded treatment of the upper chest and shoulder girdle.

Professional boundary first

The chest is a sensitive treatment area. Explain what you intend to treat, obtain specific consent, establish the draping boundary before beginning, and stay within massage therapy scope and applicable law. Breast tissue isn't part of a general chest routine.

Anatomy focus

Sternocleidomastoid
Runs from the sternum and clavicle toward the mastoid process. Palpate gently and avoid compressing the anterior triangle of the neck.
Upper trapezius
Connects the shoulder girdle and cervical region and is accessible with the client supine.
Levator scapulae
Connects the superior medial scapula with the upper cervical region.
Suboccipitals
Small muscles at the base of the skull. Support the head and use controlled, comfortable contact.
Pectoralis major
Large superficial chest muscle. Work only within clearly established professional and draping boundaries.
Pectoralis minor
Deeper structure beneath pectoralis major. Don't chase depth at the expense of comfort, safety, or boundaries.

Practice flow

  1. Position yourself at the head of the table and support the client's head comfortably.
  2. Begin with broad shoulder and upper trapezius contact.
  3. Use gentle traction and suboccipital contact while keeping the cervical spine supported.
  4. Work the lateral neck conservatively, staying aware of endangerment sites.
  5. Transition to the shoulder girdle and upper pectoral region only within the consented treatment boundary.
  6. Use broad, controlled strokes rather than aggressive pressure near the clavicle, anterior neck, or axilla.
  7. Return to the neck and shoulders with integrating strokes, then finish with still contact or gentle traction.

Endangerment-site thinking

The anterior and lateral neck contain vulnerable vascular, neural, glandular, and airway structures. Don't apply indiscriminate deep pressure over the carotid region, anterior throat, supraclavicular area, or axilla. Technique choice follows anatomy, not a desire to make every area “deep.”

Check yourself

Why is chest consent different from general consent to massage?

Because sensitive areas require clear communication about the specific area and treatment being proposed. General consent doesn't replace appropriate area-specific boundaries.

What's the priority when working the neck?

Support, anatomical awareness, comfortable pressure, and avoidance of vulnerable anterior and lateral structures.

What completes the routine?

Integration. Return from specific work to broad, calming contact so the neck, shoulders, and rest of the body feel connected rather than treated as isolated parts.
