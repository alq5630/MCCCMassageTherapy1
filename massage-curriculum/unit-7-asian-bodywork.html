Asian Bodywork Foundations

An introduction to major traditional frameworks and bodywork systems, taught with respect for their cultural histories and clear separation between traditional theory and modern biomedical evidence.

Different explanatory systems

Traditional East Asian medicine may describe health through concepts such as qi, yin and yang, meridians and five-phase theory. Ayurveda uses concepts including doshas and prana. These are traditional medical-philosophical frameworks, not anatomical structures demonstrated by modern biomedical science. Students should be able to explain the traditions without presenting metaphorical or energetic models as established anatomy.

Major approaches

Shiatsu
Japanese bodywork using sustained pressure, rhythm, positioning and stretching, often organized around traditional meridian concepts.
Thai massage
Traditionally performed clothed on a mat using compression, assisted movement and stretching.
Tui na
Chinese manual therapy using a wide range of rhythmic and focused techniques within traditional Chinese medicine.
Ayurvedic massage traditions
Indian traditions may incorporate oil, rhythm and constitutional concepts rooted in Ayurveda.

Cultural competence

Learn where a practice comes from, pronounce terminology carefully, distinguish a brief introductory exposure from formal training, and don't claim expertise in a tradition after one classroom demonstration.
