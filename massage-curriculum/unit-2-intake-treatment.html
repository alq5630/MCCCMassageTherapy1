Unit 2 · Evidence & Clinical Practice

Intake, Treatment Planning & SOAP Notes

Clinical reasoning starts before the client gets on the table. Assessment, consent, scope, communication, treatment planning, reassessment, and documentation are part of the massage.

Before massage begins

Decisions are shaped by information gathered during assessment, the client's goals and preferences, time and financial constraints, relevant evidence, and the therapist's training and expertise. Safety and scope of practice come first.

Scope of practice

Commonly within scope
Massage techniques, appropriate assessments, documentation, consent, stretching, joint mobilization, lubricants, essential oils, and hot/cold applications when permitted by applicable law and training.
Outside massage scope
Diagnosing disease and activities requiring another professional license, such as acupuncture, chiropractic/osteopathic thrust procedures, psychotherapy, or medical nutrition counseling unless separately qualified and licensed.

The client assessment

A health questionnaire should collect contact information, relevant health and medical information, and massage history/preferences. During the interview, ask why the client is seeking massage, previous massage experiences, pressure preferences, responses to recent sessions, and relevant allergies.

Exploratory
Goes deeper into the client's health, goals, or preferences.
Reflective
Confirms that you understood what the client communicated.
Open-ended
Allows the client room to explain and clarify.
Closed-ended
Requests a specific answer such as yes/no or a location.

Pain is data, not a challenge

Use pain scales when pain reduction is a treatment goal. Examples include visual analog, 0–10 numeric rating, verbal descriptor, and FACES scales. A high or unusual pain report should change your reasoning, not become an invitation to “work harder.”

Treatment planning

A treatment plan can specify techniques, areas worked or avoided, duration and frequency, assessment and reassessment methods, adjunctive therapies, home-care suggestions, referrals, and relevant observations. It should be individualized for the current client and current session.

PICO helps form an evidence-search question: Patient/Population/Problem, Intervention, Comparison/Control, Outcome.

SOAP documentation

S · Subjective
What the client reports.
O · Objective
What you observe, measure, or palpate.
A · Assessment
Your massage-therapy assessment of the session findings and response, within scope.
P · Plan
What comes next: future session approach, reassessment, home care, or referral as appropriate.

Documentation should be factual, clear, professional, and protected. Record what you observe and what the client says without inventing diagnoses or conclusions outside scope.

MBLEx check

A client says, “My neck feels tight after working at my desk.” Which SOAP category is that?

Subjective. It's information reported by the client.

You observe visibly limited cervical rotation. Which category?

Objective. It's an observable finding.

What does standard of care mean?

The level and type of care expected from a reasonably competent professional with similar training working under similar circumstances.

What is the therapist's response if a client requests something outside massage scope?

Decline that request, explain the professional boundary appropriately, and refer to an appropriate qualified professional when indicated.
