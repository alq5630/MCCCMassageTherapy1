MBLEx Core · Guidelines for Professional Practice

Run a Safe, Sustainable Practice

Professional practice combines infection control, equipment safety, therapist body mechanics, draping, recordkeeping and business fundamentals. These details are part of competent client care, not separate from it.

Professional practice has three simultaneous goals

Protect the client
Clean environment, safe equipment, appropriate draping, accurate records and sound procedures.
Protect the practitioner
Body mechanics, boundaries, PPE when indicated, workload management, insurance and injury prevention.
Protect the public
Licensure, truthful advertising, competent services, privacy and compliance with applicable laws.

Equipment and treatment-room safety

Massage table
Inspect legs, cables, hinges, face cradle and upholstery. Set an appropriate height before treatment and follow manufacturer working-weight limits.
Bolsters and positioning
Use support to reduce unnecessary strain and increase comfort. Positioning must account for pregnancy, breathing difficulty, pain, mobility and medical devices.
Linens
Use clean linens for each client. Keep clean and soiled linens separated and handle contaminated material to limit cross-contamination.
Environment
Maintain safe walking paths, appropriate lighting/temperature, accessible exits and secure storage for products and records.

Hygiene, sanitation and infection control

Hand hygiene is performed before and after client contact and whenever contamination occurs. Clean/disinfect reusable surfaces and equipment according to their use and product instructions. Gloves and other PPE are selected based on anticipated exposure, not worn as decoration.

TermMeaning
CleaningPhysical removal of soil/debris and organic material. Cleaning commonly precedes disinfection.
DisinfectionUse of an appropriate chemical/physical process to destroy many or all pathogenic microorganisms on inanimate objects, depending on the level/product.
AntisepticAgent intended for use on living tissue to reduce microorganisms.
Standard precautionsInfection-control approach applied to all clients based on potential exposure rather than assumptions about who “looks sick.”

Therapist body mechanics

Base of support
Use a stable stance and move your body rather than reaching from a fixed position.
Weight transfer
Generate pressure through body weight and controlled movement rather than isolated thumb, wrist or shoulder force.
Neutral joints
Reduce prolonged wrist deviation, excessive finger loading, elevated shoulders and repeated awkward spinal positions.
Table height
Adjust for technique, client body size and your own proportions. There is no single perfect table height for every technique.

Draping

Draping protects privacy, warmth, boundaries and access to the region being treated. Expose only the area necessary for the technique, secure the drape before moving it, explain unfamiliar draping and obtain consent. Sensitive-region work requires especially clear communication and professional boundaries.

Records

Client records
Health history, intake, consent, treatment notes, relevant communications, releases and other records associated with care.
Business records
Income/expense records, taxes, payroll when applicable, insurance, licenses, contracts, inventory and other operational documents.

Records should be accurate, timely, factual, legible/usable, appropriately secured and retained according to applicable legal and business requirements.

SOAP documentation

LetterContent
S: SubjectiveWhat the client reports: symptoms, goals, changes, relevant history.
O: ObjectiveObservable/measurable findings within scope, such as posture, ROM observations or palpatory findings.
A: AssessmentYour professional interpretation of massage-relevant findings and response, within scope. It is not a medical diagnosis.
P: PlanWhat was done and/or the plan for subsequent care, home-care suggestions within scope, referral or reassessment.

Business structures and employment language

Sole proprietorship
A business owned by one individual; the business and owner are generally not separate legal entities.
Partnership
Two or more people share business ownership according to their agreement and applicable law.
Corporation
A legal entity separate from its owners/shareholders, with formal legal and tax requirements.
LLC
A state-law business structure that can provide liability separation while allowing flexible management/tax treatment.
Employee
Works under an employer relationship; classification depends on legal tests, not merely what a contract calls the worker.
Independent contractor
Operates an independent business relationship. Worker classification rules matter because misclassification affects taxes, wages and legal responsibilities.

Insurance vocabulary

Professional liability
Coverage related to claims arising from professional services, sometimes called malpractice or errors-and-omissions coverage.
General liability
Coverage for certain claims such as bodily injury or property damage associated with business operations/premises.
Property insurance
Protects covered business property against specified losses.
Disability insurance
May replace a portion of income when a covered disability prevents work.

Marketing and business ethics

Advertising should be truthful about credentials, outcomes and services. Do not guarantee cures, misrepresent training, use protected titles you do not hold, fabricate testimonials or disclose client information for marketing without appropriate permission. Clear cancellation, payment, gift-card, tipping and refund policies reduce confusion and boundary problems.

Rapid reasoning

A therapist's wrist hurts because they drive every deep stroke through a flexed thumb. What professional-practice concept applies?

Injury prevention and body mechanics. Change the tool/contact surface, align joints, use body weight and adjust table/stance rather than repeatedly overloading the thumb.

Can a client record include your diagnosis of “sciatica” if no qualified provider diagnosed it?

No. Document the client's report and your observable findings within scope rather than creating a medical diagnosis.

What is the difference between cleaning and disinfecting?

Cleaning removes soil/debris. Disinfection uses an appropriate process/product on inanimate surfaces to destroy targeted microorganisms.

A worker is called an “independent contractor” in a contract. Does that alone establish the legal classification?

No. Classification depends on applicable legal tests and the actual working relationship.

Why does draping belong in professional practice?

It protects privacy, warmth, consent, boundaries and safe access to the treatment region.
