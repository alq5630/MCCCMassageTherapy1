CPR, AED & First Aid Readiness

Massage therapists work one-on-one with clients, sometimes behind closed doors. Emergency readiness means knowing what to do before the unexpected happens.

Pennsylvania licensure requirement

Pennsylvania requires Basic Adult CPR for massage-therapy licensure. The course must include hands-on/contact training. Online-only CPR isn't accepted by the State Board. Students should use a provider currently accepted by the Board and keep proof of certification with their professional records.

This page isn't your CPR certification

Compression technique, ventilation, AED operation and first-aid skills need hands-on instruction and assessment through the approved certification course. Follow the current sequence and protocols taught by that organization rather than memorizing an old webpage.

Know your workplace before there's an emergency

Exact address
Know what location to give emergency dispatch, including suite or floor information.
AED
Know whether one is available and exactly where it's stored.
First-aid supplies
Know where the kit is and whether supplies are stocked and current.
Emergency exits
Know how clients and staff can leave safely.
Phone access
Don't assume your personal phone will always be charged or within reach.
Facility plan
Know who calls, who meets EMS, who documents and who manages other clients.

Massage-room symptoms you don't ignore

Chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, stroke-like signs, sudden altered consciousness, severe allergic reaction, seizure, serious injury, signs of shock or other potentially life-threatening changes require you to stop the session and respond according to your training. When emergency services are indicated, don't delay because you're hoping massage, water or rest will make the problem disappear.

Fainting and sudden illness

If a client becomes faint, unresponsive or suddenly ill, stop treatment and assess according to your CPR/first-aid training. Protect them from falls, don't leave an unstable client standing, and don't automatically assume every episode is harmless. Escalate when symptoms, responsiveness or your training indicate emergency care.

Seizure awareness

Protect the person from nearby hazards and follow your current first-aid training. Don't restrain their movements or put objects in their mouth. Emergency activation depends on circumstances taught in your certification, such as prolonged or repeated seizure activity, injury, breathing problems or other concerning factors.

After an emergency

Follow facility procedures for documentation and incident reporting. Record observable facts rather than speculation. Protect client confidentiality while providing necessary information to emergency responders. Restock used emergency supplies and review whether the facility response revealed a safety gap.

Emergency-readiness walk-through

Before graduation, students should be able to walk into a massage setting and answer these questions without searching during the emergency:

  1. What's the exact address?
  2. Where is the AED?
  3. Where is the first-aid kit?
  4. How do I summon emergency services?
  5. How would EMS reach this treatment room?
  6. What information would I give dispatch?
  7. What is my role until trained responders arrive?
  8. What gets documented afterward?

Maintain certification

Track your expiration date instead of treating CPR as a one-time school requirement. Pennsylvania's Board currently lists accepted providers and specifically states that CPR must be contact/hands-on training. Verify the current list when renewing or taking a new course because Board requirements can change.
