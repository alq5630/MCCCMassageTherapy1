Theory Exam #6 Review

Business structures, worker classification, insurance, taxes, scope, licensure and long-term practice sustainability.

Fast distinctions

W-2
Employee wage statement.
1099-NEC
Common reporting form for nonemployee compensation.
Professional liability
Claims involving professional negligence.
General liability
Many premises and ordinary business liability risks.
Product liability
Harm associated with products sold or supplied.
Scope of practice
Legal and professional limits on what you may do.

Business structures

A sole proprietorship is simple but doesn't create a separate liability entity. An LLC is a legal entity created under state law and commonly uses pass-through taxation by default. An S corporation is a federal tax election, not a standalone state-law entity type.

Worker classification

Look at the real working relationship. Who controls the schedule, prices, methods, equipment, financial risk and opportunity for profit? Calling someone a contractor doesn't make them one if the business controls the relationship like employment.

Tax checkpoint

Self-employed therapists should understand estimated taxes, recordkeeping and the difference between business profit and cash sitting in a bank account. Form 1040-ES is commonly associated with federal estimated tax planning. Tax situations vary, so therapists should know when to use a qualified tax professional.
