Professional Client Communication Lab

Students should practice the words, not just understand the concept.

Pressure check

“How is this pressure for you? I can adjust lighter or deeper.”

Avoid leading the client toward “deeper is better.”

Sensitive-region consent

“I’d like to work the upper pectoral area near the shoulder because it relates to the movement we discussed. I’ll keep the chest securely draped. Is that okay with you?”

When something feels unsafe

“I’m going to stop working that area. What you’re describing is something I don’t want to aggravate with massage.”

Referral without diagnosis

“This finding is outside what I can evaluate through massage. I recommend having it assessed by an appropriate healthcare professional before we work directly there.”

Client requests inappropriate contact

“That is not part of professional massage therapy. I need to keep the session within professional boundaries.”

If behavior continues or safety/boundaries require it, end the session according to policy.

Client withdraws consent

“Absolutely. I’ll stop there and cover the area. We can move to another region or end the session.”

Explaining soreness without overclaiming

“Some people notice temporary tenderness after massage, but significant or persistent pain isn’t something you need to push through. Let me know how you respond so we can adjust next time.”

Scope-safe explanation

“I can tell you what I’m observing and how the tissue responds to massage, but diagnosing the cause of your symptoms is outside my scope.”
