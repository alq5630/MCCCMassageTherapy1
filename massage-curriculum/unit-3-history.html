History of Massage Therapy

Massage didn't emerge from one inventor or one culture. Follow the major historical threads that shaped contemporary massage and bodywork.

A very compressed timeline

Prehistoric evidence
Touch, rubbing, and manipulation likely predate written medical systems. Historical claims become more specific once written records appear.
Ancient China & India
Chinese medical traditions documented massage practices early, while massage became integrated into Ayurvedic traditions in India.
Egypt, Japan, Greece & Rome
Manual therapies traveled and evolved. Japanese amma contributed to later shiatsu traditions. Greek and Roman medical writers described massage, exercise, bathing, and friction.
Middle Ages
European medical traditions shifted after Rome's collapse, while Persian and Islamic scholars preserved and expanded important Greco-Roman medical knowledge.
Renaissance & Enlightenment
Renewed anatomical and medical study helped revive interest in manual treatment. Ambroise Paré discussed massage movements, while William Harvey's work transformed understanding of circulation.
19th century
Pehr Henrik Ling's movement system became hugely influential. Johann Mezger helped popularize French massage terminology such as effleurage, pétrissage, and tapotement.
20th century to today
Massage intersected with rehabilitation, nursing, physical therapy, wellness culture, research, licensure, professional organizations, and a rapidly expanding range of bodywork systems.

People worth knowing

Hippocrates
Ancient Greek physician whose writings supported rubbing and manual treatment.
Avicenna
Persian polymath whose Canon of Medicine became enormously influential in medical education.
Pehr Henrik Ling
Developed a Swedish system combining movement, exercise, and manual methods.
Johann Mezger
Associated massage with physical rehabilitation and French technique terminology still used today.
Florence Nightingale
Helped establish modern nursing, in a period when massage was incorporated into nursing care.
Hartvig Nissen
Established an early U.S. massage training institution and published on Swedish movement and massage.

The profession develops

Massage increasingly became organized through schools, professional associations, credentialing, research, and state regulation. Modern U.S. students should recognize organizations and institutions such as AMTA, ABMP, the Massage Therapy Foundation, NCBTMB, FSMTB, and the MBLEx, while understanding that their roles aren't interchangeable.

Why does massage history matter clinically?

It helps students distinguish historical traditions from modern evidence, understand why techniques use certain language, and see how massage became a regulated profession rather than treating current practice as if it appeared fully formed.
