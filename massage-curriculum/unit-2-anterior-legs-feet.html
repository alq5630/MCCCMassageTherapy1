Unit 2 · Practical

Anterior Legs & Feet

Turn the client supine and continue the routine through the quadriceps, lateral and medial thigh, anterior lower leg, ankle, and foot.

Setup

Client: supine with a knee bolster when useful. Therapist: archer stance alongside the thigh, transitioning toward a balanced stance at the foot. Priority: secure draping and clear boundaries around the proximal thigh.

Anatomy focus

Quadriceps
Rectus femoris and the three vasti extend the knee. Rectus femoris also crosses the hip.
Sartorius
Runs diagonally across the anterior thigh from the ASIS region toward the medial knee.
Adductor group
Located medially. Work requires conservative draping, communication, and respect for sensitive boundaries.
TFL
Small anterolateral hip muscle associated with the iliotibial tract.
Tibialis anterior
Located lateral to the tibial crest and becomes easier to identify with dorsiflexion.
Foot
Integrate the arches, metatarsal region, heel, toes, and ankle with broad contact and gentle mobilization.

Practice flow

  1. Secure the drape and establish broad contact.
  2. Warm the thigh with broad effleurage and compression.
  3. Use your stance to glide toward the hip without leaning through your wrists.
  4. Work quadriceps broadly before differentiating individual structures.
  5. Approach medial thigh work conservatively and only within the agreed treatment area.
  6. Move to the lower leg and identify tibialis anterior lateral to the tibia.
  7. Integrate the ankle and foot using broad glides, gentle traction, and appropriate mobilization.
  8. Finish the limb with lighter connecting strokes before redraping.

Safety details

Avoid direct pressure over the tibial crest, patella, superficial bony landmarks, visibly irritated tissue, and any local contraindication. Never treat the proximal medial thigh casually. The client's consent, draping, and comfort determine the treatment boundary.
