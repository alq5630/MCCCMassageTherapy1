Contraindications & Endangerment Sites

Decide when massage should be postponed, when a specific area should be avoided or modified, and where anatomy requires extra caution.

Contraindication doesn't always mean “no massage”

Absolute contraindication
The massage session should be postponed or stopped because massage isn't appropriate at that time. Examples include contagious illness, fever/systemic infection, certain acute exacerbations, or a medical emergency.
Local contraindication
Massage may still be appropriate, but a specific area is avoided or modified. Examples include acute inflammation, recent injury, suspicious skin changes, some medical devices, or an endangerment site.

When a situation is outside the massage therapist's scope or the safety implications aren't clear, the therapist doesn't diagnose. The appropriate response may include postponing, modifying, or requesting medical guidance within applicable law and policy.

Common endangerment sites

Endangerment sites are areas where vulnerable structures are relatively superficial or unprotected. They require anatomical awareness and appropriate pressure.

Anterior neck
Contains vulnerable structures including the carotid arteries, jugular veins, thyroid region, airway, and nerves. Avoid inappropriate deep/direct pressure.
Axilla
Contains lymphatic structures and major neurovascular structures including portions of the brachial plexus and vessels.
Popliteal fossa
The space behind the knee contains major vessels and nerves. Avoid deep direct pressure.
Abdomen
Contains relatively unprotected internal organs. Abdominal work requires appropriate training, consent, technique, and pressure.

Clinical decision pattern

1. Identify
What condition, symptom, tissue state, device, medication, or anatomical site matters?
2. Decide
Does it affect the entire session, only a region, positioning, pressure, technique, or timing?
3. Stay in scope
Don't diagnose or promise treatment of disease. Gather relevant information and work within massage scope.
4. Document
Record relevant client information, modifications, communication, and the work performed.

Scenario lab

Client arrives with fever and body aches. Decide what you'd do and explain why.
Client has a fresh ankle sprain but otherwise feels well. Decide whether the issue is global or local.
Client has a transdermal medication patch. Explain how you'd handle the area.
Locate the anterior neck, axilla, popliteal fossa, and abdomen on a partner without applying risky pressure.
Practice explaining a modification to a client without diagnosing them.

MBLEx check

Massage can be performed safely as long as one affected area is avoided. What type of contraindication is this?

Local contraindication.

A client has a contagious illness and fever. Local or absolute?

Absolute for that session. Postpone massage.

Why is the popliteal fossa an endangerment site?

Major blood vessels and nerves pass through the area relatively superficially, so deep direct pressure is inappropriate.

Does identifying a contraindication mean diagnosing the client's disease?

No. Massage therapists identify safety considerations and work within scope rather than diagnosing disease.
