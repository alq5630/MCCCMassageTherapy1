Medication Classes: Massage Safety Guide

Massage therapists do not prescribe, stop or change medication. The exam-relevant skill is recognizing how a medication class may change bruising, sensation, alertness, blood pressure, glucose, skin/tissue status or symptom feedback.

ClassCommon purpose/examplesMassage relevance
NSAIDsPain/inflammationCan reduce pain feedback; some increase GI/bleeding concerns. Do not use reduced pain as proof tissue can tolerate aggressive work.
AnticoagulantsReduce clot formationIncreased bruising/bleeding risk; pressure may need modification. Never advise skipping doses for massage.
Antiplatelet drugsReduce platelet aggregationBruising/bleeding consideration.
CorticosteroidsInflammatory/immune conditionsLong-term/systemic use can affect skin/tissue fragility, immunity and bone health depending on dose/duration.
Muscle relaxantsMuscle spasm/painMay cause sedation/dizziness and alter feedback.
Opioid analgesicsModerate/severe painSedation, dizziness and altered pain perception; impaired client requires safety judgment.
AntihypertensivesBlood-pressure controlSome contribute to orthostatic dizziness; allow slow position changes.
DiureticsHypertension/edema/heart conditionsFluid/electrolyte effects and increased urination; edema cause matters.
InsulinGlucose loweringHypoglycemia awareness, meal/activity timing and injection/device sites may matter.
Other glucose-lowering drugsDiabetes managementKnow hypoglycemia risk varies by class; screen neuropathy/skin/circulation complications.
AntihistaminesAllergy symptomsSome cause drowsiness/dryness.
Antidepressants/anxiolyticsMood/anxiety and other indicationsSide effects vary; some cause sedation, dizziness or autonomic effects. Do not infer diagnosis solely from medication.
AnticonvulsantsSeizures, neuropathic pain and other usesDizziness/sedation may occur; medication name does not prove why client takes it.
Thyroid replacementHypothyroidismUsually not itself a massage contraindication; underlying stability/symptoms matter.
ImmunosuppressantsAutoimmune disease/transplant and other usesInfection risk and tissue/skin effects can matter depending on drug/condition.
Chemotherapy/targeted cancer therapiesCancer treatmentPossible fatigue, neuropathy, low blood counts, skin changes, ports/lines and infection/bleeding concerns. Oncology-specific training/coordination may be appropriate.

Medication interview

Ask: What do you take it for? Any dizziness, bruising, numbness, sedation or skin effects? Any recent dose change? A medication list is useful because of its effects, not because massage therapists should interpret it like a prescriber.

Exam trap

Never tell a client to discontinue a prescribed medication so they can receive massage. When a medication creates uncertainty, modify, gather more information or ask the client to consult the prescribing healthcare professional.
