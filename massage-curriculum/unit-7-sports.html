Sports Massage

Sports massage changes with timing. The massage an athlete receives before competition shouldn't look like the massage they receive during recovery.

Four contexts

Pre-event
Brief, stimulating and specific to readiness. Avoid creating soreness, fatigue or excessive relaxation.
Inter-event
Short recovery between bouts or events. Address immediate comfort while preserving readiness.
Post-event
Recovery-focused. Screen for acute injury, dehydration, heat illness and symptoms that need medical attention before massage.
Maintenance / rehabilitative support
Work between events can be more targeted, but massage therapists still don't diagnose or independently rehabilitate injuries outside scope.

Pre-event toolbox

Use brisk, rhythmic techniques, compression, shaking, active movement and sport-specific preparation when appropriate. Keep it familiar. Competition day isn't the time to introduce an intense new technique.

Post-event priorities

First determine whether massage is appropriate. A suspected strain, sprain, fracture, concussion, exertional heat illness or other acute problem may require medical evaluation. When massage is appropriate, keep pressure and duration matched to the athlete's condition and recovery goals.

Evidence-aware claims

Massage may help perceived soreness, relaxation and short-term recovery experience. Don't promise that massage prevents all injuries, flushes lactic acid, dramatically speeds tissue healing, or guarantees better athletic performance.
