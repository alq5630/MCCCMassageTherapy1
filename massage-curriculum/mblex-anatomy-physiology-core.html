The Body Systems You Must Know

A stand-alone review of the structure and function vocabulary required to reason through massage questions. This page is the map. Each system should be studied as anatomy + physiology + massage relevance + pathology connections.

Start with organization and homeostasis

Anatomy studies structure. Physiology studies function. Structural organization progresses from chemical → cellular → tissue → organ → organ system → organism. Homeostasis is the body's maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment through regulatory processes, often involving negative feedback.

Metabolism
The total chemical reactions occurring in the body. Catabolism breaks substances down; anabolism builds them.
Cell
The basic structural and functional unit of living organisms.
Tissue
A group of similar cells and extracellular material performing related functions.
Organ
A structure composed of multiple tissue types working together.

Four primary tissue types

Epithelial
Covers surfaces, lines cavities and forms glands. Functions include protection, secretion and absorption.
Connective
Supports, binds, protects and transports. Includes fascia, tendons, ligaments, adipose, cartilage, bone and blood.
Muscle
Skeletal muscle produces voluntary movement; cardiac muscle forms the heart; smooth muscle is found in hollow organs and vessels.
Nervous
Neurons and supporting cells specialize in communication, sensation, integration and control.

Cell transport in plain language

Diffusion
Particles move from higher to lower concentration without direct cellular energy expenditure.
Osmosis
Movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane.
Facilitated diffusion
Passive movement down a gradient with help from membrane proteins.
Active transport
Moves substances against a concentration gradient using cellular energy.

Tissue injury and repair

The MBLEx explicitly includes tissue injury and repair. Students should understand the broad progression rather than treating healing as an exact calendar.

Inflammatory phase
Hemostasis and inflammatory processes begin after injury. Heat, redness, swelling and pain may be present. Aggressive local work is generally inappropriate over acutely injured tissue.
Proliferative/repair phase
Fibroblastic activity, collagen deposition and tissue rebuilding progress. New tissue is not yet fully mature.
Remodeling/maturation
Collagen and tissue organization continue changing over time as tensile capacity improves.
Clinical point
Stage, symptoms, tissue integrity, medical restrictions and function matter more than memorizing a universal “X days after injury” rule.

How this connects to massage

MBLEx questions often require combining systems. A medication can alter cardiovascular function, which changes transfer safety. A nerve problem can alter sensation, which changes pressure. Acute tissue injury changes local technique. Pregnancy changes multiple systems at once. Don't study systems as sealed boxes.

Rapid recall

Which tissue type includes fascia, tendons and ligaments?

Connective tissue.

What is the basic functional unit of skeletal muscle contraction?

The sarcomere.

Which division is associated with “rest and digest” activity?

The parasympathetic division of the autonomic nervous system.

Where does gas exchange occur in the lungs?

Primarily across the alveolar-capillary interface.

What vessels return blood toward the heart?

Veins.

What is the major functional organ of the urinary system?

The kidneys.

What is homeostasis?

Maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment through physiological regulation.

Why is acute inflammation relevant to massage?

Local heat, swelling, pain and active tissue injury can make direct or vigorous local massage inappropriate and may require postponement, local avoidance or medical evaluation depending on the presentation.
