Basic Massage Techniques

Before memorizing strokes, learn the variables that make a technique feel intentional: pressure, direction, excursion, rate, rhythm, duration, frequency, sequence, and routine.

What massage is doing mechanically

Massage manipulates soft tissue using forces such as compression and traction. Compression increases pressure on and between tissues, while traction decreases pressure by drawing structures apart. Techniques and positioning are modified according to the client's condition, comfort, goals, and response.

The variables you control

Pressure
The amount of physical force applied. It should always be appropriate for the individual and the tissue being addressed.
Direction
The path pressure travels. Many gliding and kneading movements on extremities are taught in a centripetal direction, toward the center of the body.
Excursion
The distance a technique travels, from a small focused area to the length of a muscle, limb, back, or larger body region.
Rate
How quickly a technique is repeated. Slower work tends to feel more calming, while faster work tends to feel more stimulating.
Rhythm
The regularity and predictability of movement. Smooth rhythm and uninterrupted transitions help a session feel cohesive.
Sequence & routine
A sequence is the order of techniques. A routine combines multiple techniques into a repeatable flow that can still be adapted to the client.

Pressure isn't one-size-fits-all

Light
Minimal tissue displacement. Useful for introducing touch, applying lubricant, calming work, or sensitive tissues.
Medium
More tissue engagement without forcing through resistance.
Deep
Greater tissue displacement and body-weight transfer. Deep pressure isn't automatically better and must fit the client, tissue, goal, and therapist mechanics.

Pressure should be communicated and adjusted throughout the session. Pain isn't the goal, and intensity isn't a substitute for precision.

Technique families

Effleurage
Gliding or stroking.
Petrissage
Compressing, lifting, kneading, and releasing tissue.
Friction
Rubbing or shearing movements, superficial or deep.
Compression
Pressure without gliding, sustained or rhythmic.
Tapotement
Rhythmic percussion techniques.
Vibration
Rapid trembling, shaking, or oscillating movement.

Hands-on lab

Perform the same simple gliding stroke at light, medium, and deeper pressure without changing your wrist alignment.
Repeat a stroke slowly, then more rapidly. Have your partner describe the difference in nervous-system feel.
Practice moving smoothly from one technique into another without abrupt loss of contact.
Create a three-technique mini-sequence and repeat it with consistent rhythm.
Notice whether pressure comes from muscular effort in your hands or from efficient body-weight transfer.

MBLEx check

What is the difference between rate and rhythm?

Rate is speed or repetition frequency. Rhythm is the regular pattern of the movement.

What is excursion?

The distance traveled by a massage technique.

Which direction is commonly emphasized for pressure on the extremities?

Centripetal, toward the center of the body.

What's the difference between a sequence and a routine?

A sequence is the order of techniques. A routine is a combined sequence of techniques used as a flow.
