Usui Reiki · Part Two

Develop a complete beginner session, practice self-Reiki, integrate professional boundaries and participate in the Level 1 attunement taught in class.

Self-Reiki

Self-practice gives students a way to experience stillness, hand positioning and attention before trying to interpret another person's experience. Move through comfortable positions at the head, neck, chest, abdomen and other areas as taught. Traditional associations may be used contemplatively, but they shouldn't be treated as medical diagnoses.

Settle
Choose a comfortable position and slow your breathing.
Place
Rest or hover the hands without straining wrists or shoulders.
Notice
Observe sensations and thoughts without needing to assign them a meaning.
Close
Finish deliberately and give yourself a moment to reorient.

Complete partner session

  1. Prepare a quiet, comfortable environment.
  2. Explain what Reiki is and isn't, and ask about touch preferences.
  3. Position the receiver comfortably while clothed.
  4. Center yourself with relaxed breathing.
  5. Work through the hand-position sequence taught in class.
  6. Use light touch or hands-off positioning according to consent.
  7. Allow silence and don't narrate intuitive impressions as facts.
  8. Close the session calmly and give the receiver time to reorient.
  9. Invite neutral feedback and document the service appropriately if performed professionally.

Level 1 attunement

In this course, students receive the single Level 1 attunement procedure taught by the instructor. Reiki schools and lineages don't all use identical attunement structures, so students shouldn't assume that another teacher's sequence of multiple attunements is the required method for this class.

The attunement is understood within Reiki tradition as a teacher-led spiritual or energetic initiation. Participate respectfully, but remember that an attunement isn't a medical procedure, doesn't establish a healthcare credential and doesn't authorize practice beyond the legal scope of a massage therapist.

Receiving an attunement

Follow the instructor's preparation and positioning directions. The experience can feel meaningful, ordinary, emotional, quiet, warm, tingly or like nothing noticeable at all. There isn't a required sensation that proves an attunement “worked.” Students don't need to perform an experience for the class.

Professional integration

Consent
Ask before touch and respect hands-off preferences.
Scope
Don't diagnose illness through energy or chakras.
Claims
Don't guarantee healing or replace needed medical care.
Beliefs
Never require a client to adopt the practitioner's spiritual worldview.

Skills check: 20-minute Reiki session

Students will prepare the space, explain Reiki in neutral language, obtain consent, center themselves, demonstrate an organized hand-position sequence, maintain comfortable body mechanics, respect silence, close the session appropriately and invite neutral feedback.

Reflection: After the session, write what you noticed about your own attention, body mechanics and tendency to interpret sensations. Separate observation from interpretation.

What competency looks like

By the end of Level 1, you should be able to describe Reiki's traditional framework respectfully, perform a basic self-practice and partner session, explain the role of attunement in your training, maintain consent and boundaries, and clearly distinguish spiritual tradition from medical evidence.
