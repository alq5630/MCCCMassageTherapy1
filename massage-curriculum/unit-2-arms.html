Arms & Hands

The upper-extremity sequence connects the shoulder girdle to the forearm and hand while giving students repeated practice with support, joint positioning, broad strokes, and detailed palpation.

Setup

Client: supine, arm supported at the side or positioned comfortably across the torso as appropriate. Therapist: balanced stance at the side of the table or seated when that improves control. Never let the client's arm hang unsupported.

Anatomy focus

Deltoid
Caps the shoulder and contributes to arm movement in multiple directions.
Biceps brachii
Anterior arm muscle associated with elbow flexion and forearm supination.
Triceps brachii
Posterior arm muscle and primary elbow extensor.
Forearm flexors
Predominantly anterior/medial forearm group involved in wrist and finger flexion.
Forearm extensors
Predominantly posterior/lateral group involved in wrist and finger extension.
Hand
Thenar and hypothenar regions, palm, metacarpals, digits, and intrinsic hand musculature.

Practice flow

  1. Support the arm and establish contact from shoulder to hand.
  2. Warm the upper arm with broad gliding and kneading.
  3. Work around the deltoid and upper arm without compressing vulnerable structures in the axilla.
  4. Support the wrist while working the forearm flexor and extensor compartments.
  5. Use broad palm work before more specific thumb or fingertip work in the hand.
  6. Mobilize fingers and wrist gently within comfortable range.
  7. Finish with a full-arm connecting stroke and replace the arm comfortably.

Therapist mechanics

Keep your shoulders down, wrists neutral, and the client's limb supported. Small hand muscles fatigue quickly when you pinch or overuse the thumbs. Use broad contact whenever it can accomplish the same goal more comfortably.
