Unit 2 · Practical · New Routine

Arms

The New Routine gives this region eight memorable landmarks, beginning with a full-arm sweep and ending with a final connecting stroke and redrape. Learn the sequence while understanding what lies under your hands.

Eight routine landmarks

Arm Sweep → Palm Press → Finger Weave → Forearm Strip → Arm Extend → Bicep Knead → Pec Glide → Final Sweep. These are the names used in the New Routine material, so they're the memory anchors for this practical.

Anatomy map

Biceps brachii
Crosses the shoulder and elbow. It flexes the elbow and strongly supinates the forearm. Its long and short heads join into a common distal tendon.
Brachialis
Deep to biceps and a powerful elbow flexor regardless of forearm position.
Triceps brachii
Primary elbow extensor on the posterior arm. The long head also crosses the shoulder.
Forearm flexor-pronator region
Predominantly anterior/medial. Many superficial flexors share attachment near the medial epicondyle.
Forearm extensor-supinator region
Predominantly posterior/lateral. Many superficial extensors share attachment near the lateral epicondyle.
Pectoralis major
Large anterior chest muscle inserting on the humerus. It contributes to adduction and medial rotation and has clavicular and sternocostal portions.
Palm
Thenar and hypothenar eminences frame the central palm. The hand contains dense sensory innervation, so pressure should be specific but responsive.
Wrist & fingers
Numerous tendons cross the wrist into the hand. Support joints during traction and movement rather than pulling from unsupported digits.

New Routine sequence

Arm Sweep
Perform effleurage from the wrist toward the shoulder. Use broad contact to connect the entire limb, establish pressure tolerance and prepare the arm for more specific work.
Palm Press
Use thumb pressure into the palm as demonstrated. Work methodically through the palm rather than poking randomly. Support the dorsal hand with the opposite hand so the client's wrist isn't forced into extension.
Finger Weave
Interlock/weave the fingers and add wrist traction according to the classroom demonstration. The movement should feel supported and controlled, never like pulling on individual fingers.
Forearm Strip
Strip the forearm from wrist toward elbow. Orient to the flexor and extensor muscle masses and stay aware of superficial bony landmarks at the wrist and elbow.
Arm Extend
Rotate and extend the arm outward into the taught supported position. Move the limb within comfortable range and watch the client's shoulder rather than treating the arm like a lever.
Bicep Knead
Knead the biceps and perform the taught pin-and-stretch. Distinguish the biceps muscle belly from the cubital-fossa region and keep focused pressure out of the anterior elbow.
Pec Glide
Use an elbow or soft-fist glide across the pectoral region exactly within the draping, consent and treatment boundaries demonstrated in class. Broad, controlled contact is more important than maximal depth.
Final Sweep
Finish with effleurage through the arm and redrape. Make the ending intentional so the client experiences a clear transition before you move to the next region.

Palpation before pressure

Biceps
Ask for gentle elbow flexion with the forearm supinated. Feel the muscle belly contract on the anterior arm.
Brachialis
Palpate on either side of distal biceps where deeper brachialis can become accessible, especially with elbow flexion.
Forearm flexors
Ask for gentle wrist flexion. Follow the muscle mass proximally toward the medial epicondyle without pressing directly on the epicondyle.
Forearm extensors
Ask for gentle wrist extension and compare the posterior/lateral forearm with the flexor side.
Pectoralis major
Use its clavicular/sternal orientation and humeral action to understand the tissue direction. Palpation follows professional chest draping and consent rules.
Cubital fossa
Know it as an anterior-elbow endangerment region. Don't confuse a space containing vulnerable structures with a muscle target.

Body mechanics & limb handling

Support the limb
The client's arm shouldn't hang while you work. Support its weight with your body, table or hands as appropriate.
Arm Sweep
Move your center of mass along the table. Keep elbows soft instead of reaching with locked arms.
Palm Press
Reinforce or alternate thumbs before fatigue appears. Thumb pain isn't part of learning good massage mechanics.
Forearm Strip
Keep wrist aligned behind the contact tool and follow the muscle rather than scraping over ulna or radius.
Arm Extend
Control the shoulder and elbow throughout the movement. Joint movement is guided, not forced.
Pec Glide
Position your body so the elbow/soft fist can glide from weight transfer. Don't reach across the client or drop concentrated pressure onto ribs.

Safety & professional boundaries

Axilla: avoid deep direct pressure because of important neurovascular and lymphatic structures. Cubital fossa: don't use focused deep stripping through the anterior elbow. Medial upper arm: use anatomical awareness and appropriate pressure. Chest/pectoral work: requires explicit consent, appropriate draping, clear professional boundaries and adherence to applicable scope/law and classroom protocol.

Modify for acute injury, inflammation, wounds, altered sensation, recent surgery, unexplained swelling or any other local contraindication. Joint traction and movement should remain comfortable and within available range.

Practical checkoff

Recite all eight New Routine arm landmarks in order.
Perform Arm Sweep wrist → shoulder with continuous broad contact.
Support the hand correctly during Palm Press.
Demonstrate Finger Weave and wrist traction without pulling individual digits.
Identify flexor and extensor forearm regions before Forearm Strip.
Move through Arm Extend while supporting the shoulder and elbow.
Demonstrate Bicep Knead and the taught pin/stretch while protecting the cubital fossa.
Perform Pec Glide only within correct consent and draping boundaries.
Finish with a complete Final Sweep and redrape.

MBLEx + clinical reasoning

Which muscle both flexes the elbow and strongly supinates the forearm?

Biceps brachii.

Which muscle is a strong elbow flexor regardless of forearm position?

Brachialis.

Why should deep focused pressure be avoided in the cubital fossa?

It's an endangerment area containing important neurovascular structures rather than a muscle belly intended for routine deep stripping.

Why support the dorsal hand during Palm Press?

It stabilizes the hand and prevents thumb pressure from simply forcing the wrist into uncomfortable extension.

A client's shoulder resists Arm Extend and they report pinching. What should the therapist do?

Stop or reduce the movement and reassess. The routine doesn't override the client's available comfortable range.

What major muscle is being addressed during Pec Glide?

Pectoralis major, within appropriate consent and draping boundaries.
