Nervous System Pathology Master

ConditionRecognizeMassage reasoning
Peripheral neuropathyNumbness, tingling, pain or weakness from peripheral nerve dysfunction; causes varyReduced sensation changes pressure/heat safety. Protect skin and do not rely solely on feedback.
Carpal tunnel syndromeMedian nerve compression at carpal tunnelMay include thumb/index/middle sensory symptoms and weakness. Avoid provoking neural symptoms.
Ulnar neuropathyUlnar nerve irritation/compression, commonly cubital tunnel/wristProtect cubital tunnel; tingling/electric symptoms are not a target for deeper compression.
RadiculopathyNerve-root dysfunction causing pain/sensory/motor/reflex changesProgressive weakness or major neurological change warrants referral; avoid repeatedly reproducing radiating symptoms.
SciaticaCommon term for symptoms along sciatic distribution, often from lumbosacral nerve-root irritationIt is a symptom pattern, not automatically piriformis syndrome. Screen neurological/red-flag features.
Multiple sclerosisImmune-mediated CNS demyelinating diseaseAdapt to fatigue, heat sensitivity, sensation, spasticity and mobility.
Parkinson diseaseProgressive neurodegenerative movement disorderRigidity, tremor, bradykinesia, balance and medication timing affect positioning/transfers.
StrokeAcute brain injury from vascular interruption/bleedingNew FAST-type signs are emergency. Chronic post-stroke work considers sensation, tone, weakness and communication.
Seizure disorderRecurrent predisposition to seizuresKnow history/triggers and emergency plan. Do not restrain convulsive movement or place objects in mouth.
MigraineNeurological headache disorder, often with nausea/light/sound sensitivity; aura may occurEnvironment/pressure tolerance matter. Sudden unusual severe headache or neurological deficit needs evaluation.
Bell palsyAcute peripheral facial nerve palsyMedical evaluation is important for new facial weakness because stroke must be distinguished clinically.
Trigeminal neuralgiaSevere episodic facial pain in trigeminal distributionLight touch may trigger pain; do not assume deeper facial massage is helpful.

Neurological red flags

New/progressive weakness, sudden facial droop or speech difficulty, loss of bowel/bladder control with major neurological symptoms, altered consciousness, severe sudden headache, or rapidly changing neurological function requires escalation beyond routine massage.
