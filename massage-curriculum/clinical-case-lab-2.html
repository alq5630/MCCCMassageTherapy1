Clinical Reasoning Case Lab · Set 2

1 · Hot stone + neuropathy

Client with diabetic peripheral neuropathy cannot reliably distinguish warm from hot on the feet and requests hot stones there.

Best reasoning: Avoid unsafe thermal application to areas with unreliable sensation. Modify location/modality; screen skin and vascular status.

2 · RA flare

Client with rheumatoid arthritis says both wrists are unusually hot, swollen and painful today.

Best reasoning: Avoid aggressive local work on acutely inflamed joints; adapt the session or postpone affected-region work depending overall status.

3 · Stable OA

Older client with diagnosed knee OA has chronic stiffness but no acute swelling/redness and wants relaxation massage.

Best reasoning: OA is not an automatic contraindication. Position comfortably, work to tolerance and avoid forcing painful ROM.

4 · Shingles

Client has painful blistering rash in a band on one side of torso and says doctor diagnosed shingles yesterday.

Best reasoning: Avoid active lesions/affected region and consider transmission/infection-control implications. A routine session may need postponement depending presentation.

5 · New facial droop

Client arrives saying one side of face suddenly feels weak and smile looks uneven.

Best reasoning: Possible stroke until appropriately evaluated. Emergency response, not facial massage.

6 · Migraine history

Client has familiar migraine with light sensitivity, no unusual neurological symptoms, and asks for quiet massage.

Best reasoning: If presentation is typical for them and otherwise safe, adapt environment/position/pressure. New or unusual severe neurological features change the decision.

7 · Fibromyalgia

Client says prior therapist insisted massage must hurt to 'break knots' and it caused a flare for two days.

Best reasoning: Individualize pressure. Pain is not proof of effectiveness. Use client response and goals to dose treatment.

8 · Kidney disease edema

Client with advanced kidney disease has increased bilateral leg swelling and asks you to push fluid upward.

Best reasoning: Do not assume edema is a local mechanical problem. Medical condition/fluid status requires appropriate guidance; avoid unsupported drainage claims.

9 · Fresh tattoo

Large tattoo completed yesterday is red/tender and covered according to artist instructions.

Best reasoning: Treat it as healing skin/wound. Avoid direct massage/product contamination and follow appropriate skin-care boundaries.

10 · Cupping + anticoagulant

Client on anticoagulant requests strong suction because they like dark marks.

Best reasoning: Bruising/bleeding risk plus concentrated suction means modify/avoid as appropriate. Darker marks are not a therapeutic target.

11 · Sexual comment

Client makes repeated sexual comments after being redirected once.

Best reasoning: Maintain professional boundary; if behavior continues or policy/safety warrants, terminate session and document objectively.

12 · Confidentiality

A client's spouse calls asking what the client told you during intake.

Best reasoning: Do not disclose without appropriate authorization unless a specific legal exception applies.

13 · Contractor classification

Spa controls therapist schedule, pricing, procedures and many details but calls everyone independent contractors.

Best reasoning: The label alone does not determine legal classification. Applicable legal tests and actual relationship matter.

14 · Abdominal pain

Client has sudden severe right-lower abdominal pain, nausea and guarding.

Best reasoning: Do not perform deep abdominal massage. Acute severe unexplained abdominal symptoms need medical evaluation.

15 · Postpartum calf

Two weeks postpartum client reports one calf became swollen and painful this morning.

Best reasoning: Postpartum VTE risk plus unilateral calf symptoms = prompt medical evaluation; no leg massage.

16 · Open athlete's foot lesion

Client has peeling, cracked, itchy skin between toes consistent with active fungal infection.

Best reasoning: Avoid affected feet/region and prevent cross-contamination. Do not spread product or massage across lesions.

17 · Scar request

Client wants deep scar work three days after abdominal surgery.

Best reasoning: Too early for direct scar work. Protect healing incision and follow surgical restrictions.

18 · Scope

Client asks which blood-pressure medication they should stop because it makes them dizzy.

Best reasoning: Do not recommend medication changes. Encourage discussion with prescribing clinician; manage immediate positional safety.

19 · Treatment response

Client reports their shoulder felt worse for three days after the last aggressive session.

Best reasoning: Use that response as treatment-planning data. Reassess goals, reduce/change approach rather than repeating because 'deep work takes time.'

20 · Unknown allergy

Client reports prior rash from scented lotion but does not know ingredient.

Best reasoning: Use a product with known tolerated ingredients or avoid topical exposure; do not experiment during session.
