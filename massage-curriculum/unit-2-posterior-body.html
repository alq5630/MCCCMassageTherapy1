Glutes & Posterior Legs

Expand the prone routine from the back into the hip, posterior thigh, lower leg, and foot while keeping draping, body mechanics, anatomy, and client comfort integrated.

Setup

Client: prone with ankles bolstered. Therapist: archer stance near the hips and legs, changing sides as needed. Focus: broad warming, muscular decompression, hip and leg mobility, and a continuous transition from the back into the lower body.

Know what you're touching

Gluteus maximus
Large superficial hip extensor. Use broad contact and work through appropriate draping when indicated.
Gluteus medius
Lateral hip muscle important for abduction and pelvic stability.
Piriformis
Deep lateral rotator located between the sacral region and greater trochanter beneath gluteus maximus.
Hamstrings
Biceps femoris laterally; semitendinosus and semimembranosus medially. Follow them from the ischial region toward the posterior knee without compressing the popliteal space.
Gastrocnemius & soleus
Posterior calf muscles. Gastrocnemius is more superficial; soleus lies deeper.
Plantar fascia
Dense connective tissue along the sole of the foot.

Technique sequence

  1. Transition from the back with broad contact and grounding compression.
  2. Warm the gluteal region with palmar compression and slow rocking.
  3. Use broad forearm work where appropriate rather than forcing pressure through thumbs or wrists.
  4. Warm the posterior thigh with broad gliding and kneading before more specific work.
  5. Differentiate the hamstrings as your palpation skill develops. Keep pressure away from the popliteal fossa.
  6. Work the calf broadly, then differentiate gastrocnemius from deeper soleus.
  7. Integrate the foot with broad contact and plantar work as appropriate.
  8. Finish with lighter connecting strokes and maintain contact during transitions.

Body mechanics + draping

Use an archer stance and transfer body weight through the legs and hips. Keep wrists neutral during forearm or elbow work. Secure the drape before exposing a leg and uncover only the area you're actively treating. Gluteal work requires especially clear consent, professional boundaries, and secure draping.

Palpation lab

How can you distinguish the medial and lateral hamstrings near the knee?

Biceps femoris forms the more lateral hamstring tendon. Semitendinosus and semimembranosus are medial.

Where should deep pressure be avoided behind the knee?

The popliteal fossa. It contains vulnerable neurovascular structures and is an endangerment site.

What should happen to your stance when you want more pressure?

Improve your leverage and body-weight transfer rather than muscling downward with your arms.

Why alternate specific work with broad strokes?

Broad contact helps warm and integrate the region, lets you reassess tissue response, and creates a smoother transition around more focused work.
