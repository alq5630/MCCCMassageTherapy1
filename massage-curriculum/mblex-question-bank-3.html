Integrated MBLEx Question Bank · Set 3

Thirty application questions. Answer before opening the explanation.

1. A receptor responding to muscle length/change in length is the: A GTO · B muscle spindle · C nociceptor only · D SA node

B. Muscle spindles provide information about muscle length and change in length.

2. Which structure contributes most directly to hearing transduction? A Cochlear hair cells · B Thyroid follicles · C Nephrons · D Osteons

A. Cochlear hair cells convert mechanical vibration into neural signaling.

3. Semicircular canals are most associated with: A blood pressure · B rotational head movement/balance · C digestion · D glucose storage

B. They are part of the vestibular apparatus.

4. Pain and nociception are: A identical · B unrelated · C related but not identical · D always proportional to tissue damage

C. Pain is an experience influenced by context; nociception is neural processing of potentially harmful stimuli.

5. Meridians are most associated with: A traditional Chinese medicine · B renal anatomy · C cardiac conduction · D skeletal histology

A. Meridians are traditional energetic concepts.

6. Doshas belong primarily to: A Ayurveda · B Western cardiology · C lymphatic anatomy · D Swedish stroke taxonomy

A. Vata, pitta and kapha are Ayurvedic concepts.

7. A client suddenly develops facial droop and slurred speech. Best response? A massage neck · B wait 30 minutes · C activate emergency care · D apply heat

C. Sudden focal neurological signs can indicate stroke.

8. During massage a client develops severe chest pressure and shortness of breath. Best response? A finish session · B deep pec work · C emergency response · D stretch shoulder

C. Potential cardiac emergency overrides the massage plan.

9. During a convulsive seizure you should: A restrain limbs · B place object in mouth · C protect from nearby hazards · D give water

C. Do not restrain or place objects in the mouth.

10. Which term means objective evidence observable by another person? A symptom · B sign · C prognosis · D etiology

B. Symptoms are subjectively experienced; signs are objectively observed/measured.

11. Etiology refers to: A predicted course · B cause/origin · C treatment plan · D number of old cases

B.

12. Incidence refers primarily to: A all existing cases · B new cases in a population/time period · C deaths only · D symptoms only

B. Prevalence counts existing cases; incidence tracks new cases.

13. A fomite is: A a lymphocyte · B contaminated object capable of indirect transmission · C hormone · D muscle receptor

B.

14. Standard precautions are used: A only for known HIV · B only in hospitals · C routinely regardless of known infection status · D only when gloves are worn

C. They are baseline infection-control practices.

15. Hands coated in massage lubricant should generally be cleaned with: A more lubricant · B soap and water · C dry towel only · D perfume

B. Product/soil can reduce sanitizer effectiveness.

16. A visibly dirty treatment surface should generally be: A disinfected without cleaning regardless of label · B cleaned, then appropriately disinfected · C covered forever · D ignored

B. Organic soil can interfere with disinfection.

17. A client has an acute contagious systemic illness. Best category? A routine deep massage · B postpone · C local foot avoidance only · D increase heat

B. Systemic contagious illness commonly warrants postponement.

18. A small healing localized wound most often represents: A local consideration · B mandatory whole-body deep massage · C no infection-control concern · D diagnosis opportunity

A. Protect/avoid the area and manage contamination risk.

19. Which is most concerning for neural irritation? A broad comfortable pressure · B electrical radiating pain · C mild warmth · D relaxation

B. Stop or modify and reassess.

20. A massage therapist sees an unfamiliar skin lesion. Appropriate action? A diagnose melanoma · B describe objectively and recommend evaluation when warranted · C remove it · D promise it is benign

B. Stay within scope.

21. Which is a portal-of-exit concept in infection transmission? A route pathogen leaves reservoir · B muscle attachment · C joint capsule · D business record

A.

22. Which transmission involves a contaminated object? A direct contact · B indirect contact · C genetic · D endocrine

B.

23. Which transmission involves an insect/animal carrier? A vector · B osmosis · C fomite only · D endocrine

A.

24. The safest response to enlarged painful lymph nodes is: A aggressively knead them · B treat as trigger points · C avoid aggressive manipulation and gather information · D use percussion

C. Nodes are not muscular knots.

25. A client withdraws consent mid-technique. Therapist should: A finish technique · B stop/modify immediately · C cite signed intake · D charge extra

B. Consent is ongoing.

26. A therapist promises massage will cure diabetes. Main problem? A good marketing · B unsupported claim and scope issue · C required informed consent · D standard precaution

B. Massage does not cure diabetes.

27. SOAP 'O' contains: A client-reported feelings only · B objective observations/findings · C future plan only · D advertising

B.

28. Which MBLEx domain has the largest current approximate weight? A Client Assessment/Reassessment/Treatment Planning · B A&P · C Kinesiology · D none

A. The current outline assigns it approximately 17%.

29. A client with sensory loss asks for very hot hydrotherapy because they 'can't feel much.' Best response? A hotter is safer · B sensory loss increases burn risk, modify/avoid as appropriate · C ignore it · D numbness protects skin

B. Feedback may be unreliable.

30. Which statement best separates tradition and anatomy? A chakras are dissectible glands · B meridians are arteries · C energetic systems can be taught as traditional frameworks without claiming they are biomedical structures · D qi is a cranial nerve

C. Respect the framework while maintaining accurate biomedical language.
