SOAP Notes & Documentation Lab

S · Subjective

What the client reports: symptoms, goals, changes since last visit, response to prior treatment, relevant functional complaints. Example: “Client reports right upper-back tightness after two days of desk work; 4/10 discomfort when turning right.”

O · Objective

What you observe/measure within scope: posture, gait, ROM result, visible swelling/color, palpation response, treatment performed. Avoid presenting subjective palpation impressions as proven pathology.

A · Assessment

Your professional interpretation of the session information within massage scope. Keep it tied to treatment planning rather than medical diagnosis. Example: “Right cervical rotation limited by reported tightness; tolerated broad upper-back/neck work without neurological symptoms.”

P · Plan

What happens next: home-care education within scope, reassessment, future session focus, modifications, referral or follow-up. Example: “Reassess comfortable cervical rotation next visit; continue pressure within tolerance; client advised to seek medical evaluation for any new weakness/numbness.”

Complete example

S: Client reports 4/10 upper-back tension after increased computer work, denies injury/numbness/tingling. Goal is easier head turning.

O: Comfortable right cervical rotation appears less than left. Increased tenderness reported with moderate pressure to right upper trapezius. Performed Swedish massage and gentle ROM within comfort.

A: Client tolerated session well; right rotation felt easier afterward by client report. No adverse response observed.

P: Reassess next visit. Continue goal-directed upper-back/neck work if presentation remains appropriate.

Avoid

“Broke up adhesions.” “Released toxins.” “Diagnosed pinched nerve.” “Fixed scoliosis.” “Client was difficult.” Documentation should be objective, respectful, clinically useful and within scope.
