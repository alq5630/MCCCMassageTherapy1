Theory Exam #4 Review

Condition-specific massage, special populations, pharmacology, and the clinical decisions that connect all three.

How to study this exam

Don't memorize a list of diseases and random massage rules. For each condition, know what it is, what makes massage risky, what findings change the plan, what you would modify, and what would make you stop and refer.

Condition quick review

Fibromyalgia
Widespread pain and tenderness with variable sensitivity. Start conservatively, avoid provoking a flare, and individualize pressure.
Migraine vs tension headache
Tension-type headaches commonly involve pressure/tightness. Migraine can include throbbing pain, nausea, and light/sound sensitivity. Modify the environment and avoid treating a presentation that needs medical evaluation.
Strain
Injury to muscle or tendon. Acute injury calls for protection and avoidance of aggressive local work. Later stages may tolerate gradual massage and movement.
Spasm & cramp
Involuntary contraction. Recurrent unexplained cramping, neurological changes, severe swelling, or systemic symptoms warrant referral.
Osteoarthritis
Degenerative joint disease. Work for comfort and surrounding mobility, but don't aggressively stress an acutely irritated joint.
Tendinitis / tendinopathy
Acute reactive tissue and chronic tendon changes require different loading. Avoid aggressive friction on acutely inflamed tissue.
Plantar heel pain
Often worst with first steps after rest. Address relevant calf and foot tissues without aggressively irritating a painful attachment site.
Disc conditions / sciatica
Sciatica describes nerve-related symptoms, not one specific cause. Stop for progressive weakness, bowel/bladder changes, saddle anesthesia, or other concerning neurological findings.
Shin splints
Overuse-related lower-leg pain. Don't pound directly into acute irritated tissue. Reduce aggravation and address contributing soft-tissue tension when appropriate.
Carpal tunnel syndrome
Median nerve compression at the wrist. Consider the whole upper extremity while avoiding aggressive pressure that increases neurological symptoms.
Thoracic outlet syndrome
Compression involving neurovascular structures between the neck and upper extremity. Neurological or vascular symptoms require caution and appropriate referral.
Varicose veins
Avoid deep direct pressure over affected veins. Work gently around the region when otherwise appropriate.
Peripheral neuropathy
Reduced or altered sensation makes pressure feedback less reliable. Use conservative pressure and monitor tissue response.
Rheumatoid arthritis
Systemic autoimmune inflammatory disease. Avoid aggressive work over actively inflamed joints and adapt to fatigue, pain, and medications.

Pregnancy & special populations

What is supine hypotensive syndrome?
A drop in blood pressure associated with compression of major abdominal vessels by the pregnant uterus. Later pregnancy often calls for side-lying, semireclining, or an appropriate lateral tilt.
What does relaxin change?
It contributes to increased connective-tissue laxity, so aggressive joint mobilization and traction aren't appropriate choices.
Who performs infant massage?
The parent or caregiver. The therapist/instructor teaches and supports them.
What matters with minors?
Guardian consent plus the child's assent, developmentally appropriate communication, privacy, and clear professional safeguards.

Pharmacology lightning round

ADME
Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion.
Fastest route
Intravenous administration delivers medication directly into circulation.
Oral route
Generally slower and affected by digestion, absorption, food, and first-pass metabolism.
Anticoagulants
Increase concern for bruising and bleeding, so pressure may need modification.
Muscle relaxants / opioids
Reduced sensation, dizziness, or drowsiness can change pressure and transfer safety.
Beta blockers
Reduce sympathetic effects and can lower heart rate and blood pressure.
Bronchodilators
Relax airway smooth muscle to improve airflow.
Medication sites
Don't massage directly over transdermal patches, infusion devices, pellets, or recent injection sites.
