Theory Exam #3 · Cumulative Midterm Review

This midterm intentionally revisits the theory taught for Exams #1 and #2. It tests whether you can connect safety, technique, research, scope, assessment, consent and documentation instead of studying each chapter in isolation.

Alignment correction: MLD and massage history are not included in this Theory Exam #3 review. MLD is taught immediately before the midterm but wasn't part of the original cumulative Theory #3 study-guide blueprint, and Chapter 1 history is scheduled after the review. Those topics remain in the curriculum and are assessed later where appropriate.

Tools, environment & safety

Review table setup and height, linen separation, lubricant contamination prevention, therapist hygiene, handwashing, PPE, cleaning versus disinfection, Standard Precautions, absolute versus local contraindications, and the major endangerment sites. Be able to explain why each practice breaks a transmission or injury pathway.

A client arrives with fever and flu-like symptoms. What's the best decision?

Postpone the session because acute systemic illness is an absolute contraindication for routine massage that day.

Why shouldn't you scoop lubricant from a communal container with bare hands?

It can contaminate the product and turn the container into a transmission source.

Body mechanics, positioning & draping

Know archer and horse stances, body-weight transfer, neutral spine/wrists, table-height effects, prone/supine/side-lying/semi-reclined positioning, bolsters, turning procedures and the purpose of draping.

Why is body-weight transfer preferable to muscling through deep pressure?

It improves leverage and reduces unnecessary repetitive strain through the hands, wrists, shoulders and back.

Massage techniques & effects

Know the defining movement and general intent of effleurage, petrissage, friction, compression, tapotement and vibration. Also review pressure, direction, rate, rhythm and excursion. Don't memorize exaggerated “detox” claims. Connect technique choices to realistic treatment goals and client response.

Which technique family involves kneading, lifting and squeezing?

Petrissage.

Which autonomic division is associated with rest-and-digest relaxation?

The parasympathetic nervous system.

Research & evidence

Understand that research findings can be mixed and that a reported association isn't a guaranteed mechanism. Review commonly studied outcomes such as pain, range of motion, muscle activity, edema, anxiety, stress, sleep and well-being.

What can decreased EMG activity suggest?

Reduced measured muscle electrical activity, which may be consistent with decreased activation or increased relaxation in the studied context.

Scope, intake & treatment planning

Assessment helps you plan massage, but it doesn't authorize medical diagnosis. Know open, closed, exploratory and reflective questions; common pain scales; treatment goals; PICO; reassessment; referral; and home-care recommendations that remain within massage scope.

What does PICO stand for?

Patient/population/problem, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome.

A client asks you to diagnose a shoulder injury. What should you do?

Explain the scope boundary, document massage-relevant findings and refer for medical evaluation when appropriate.

Consent & documentation

Consent is informed, voluntary and ongoing. A client can change their mind at any time. SOAP documentation should distinguish what the client reports from what the therapist observes, summarize massage-relevant assessment without diagnosing, and record the future plan.

“Client reports neck pain 6/10” belongs where in SOAP?

Subjective.

“Right shoulder appears elevated compared with left” belongs where?

Objective.

A client signed consent but refuses chest work today. What controls?

The client's current refusal. Consent can be withdrawn at any time.

How to study for a cumulative exam

Don't just reread. Explain each concept out loud, then answer a new scenario. Ask yourself: Is massage appropriate? Is the area safe? Am I inside scope? Did I obtain consent? What technique fits the goal? How would I document it? That's the reasoning chain this midterm is designed to test.
