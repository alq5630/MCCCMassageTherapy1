Theory Exam #3 · Cumulative Midterm Review

This is the first big integration point. Review the foundations, clinical reasoning, practical routine, lymphatic drainage, and massage history as one connected body of knowledge.

Midterm map

Foundations
Terminology, body orientation, tools, body mechanics, positioning, draping, hygiene, precautions, contraindications, endangerment sites, and Swedish techniques.
Clinical practice
Research literacy, intake, informed consent, treatment planning, SOAP notes, scope, reflexology, and practical decision-making.
Full-body routine
Back, posterior body, anterior legs and feet, arms and hands, neck and chest, transitions, draping, and therapist mechanics.
Lymphatic drainage
System anatomy, lymph flow, node regions, ducts, watersheds, light-pressure principles, sequence, safety, and referral thinking.
History
Major cultures, eras, figures, professional development, organizations, credentialing, and the evolution of massage terminology.
Reasoning
Be ready to choose the safest answer, not merely the technique that sounds most therapeutic.

Scenario drill

A student leans over the table and presses harder using shoulder strength. What's the first problem?

Body mechanics. Pressure should be generated efficiently through stance, weight transfer, and appropriate tools rather than sustained muscular effort through the shoulders and wrists.

A client reports fever and an acute systemic infection but wants a relaxing massage.

Defer the session. Relaxation isn't a reason to ignore a systemic contraindication.

A therapist documents “client has rotator cuff tendinitis” based only on the massage intake.

That's outside massage scope because it states a medical diagnosis. Document the client's report and massage-relevant findings instead.

During MLD practice, a student uses deep stripping strokes toward the axilla.

The direction alone doesn't make the technique MLD. Deep stripping is inconsistent with the light superficial skin-stretch used in MLD.

A tender reflex point is found under the foot. What can you conclude?

You can document tenderness. You can't diagnose dysfunction of a corresponding organ from the reflex point.

Which historical figure is strongly associated with the Swedish movement system?

Pehr Henrik Ling.

Study strategy

Study in passes. First, make sure you can define the vocabulary. Second, explain the anatomy or principle behind it. Third, apply it to a client scenario. Finally, practice eliminating answers that are unsafe, outside scope, poorly documented, or based on exaggerated claims. That's the level of thinking the cumulative exam should reward.
