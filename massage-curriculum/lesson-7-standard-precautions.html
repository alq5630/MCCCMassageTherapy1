Standard Precautions

Use routine infection-control practices consistently rather than waiting until you know a client has an infection.

What standard precautions are

Standard precautions are infection-control measures used to reduce exposure to potentially infectious material. In massage practice, they shape hand hygiene, use of personal protective equipment, linen handling, surface disinfection, and management of reusable tools.

The mindset: don't rely on appearance to decide whether hygiene matters. Safe practices are built into every session.

Core practices

Hand hygiene
Wash thoroughly with soap and water before and after client contact and whenever hands are visibly soiled. Alcohol-based sanitizer can be useful when appropriate and hands aren't visibly dirty.
PPE
Gloves, masks, gowns, and eye protection are selected based on anticipated exposure to blood, body fluids, broken skin, or other hazards.
Linens
Use clean linens for each client. Handle used linens in a way that limits unnecessary contact and launder them appropriately before reuse.
Surfaces
Clean and disinfect treatment surfaces and frequently touched items between clients using an appropriate product and its required contact time.
Reusable tools
Clean and disinfect reusable equipment according to the item and manufacturer's instructions before using it again.
Therapist health
A therapist who may transmit illness can become the reservoir in the chain of infection. Rescheduling is sometimes the professional safety decision.

Clean vs. disinfect

Cleaning physically removes soil and organic material. Disinfection uses an appropriate agent to destroy many disease-causing microorganisms on surfaces. A visibly dirty surface generally needs cleaning before effective disinfection.

Always follow current product labeling and facility policies for dilution, contact time, compatible surfaces, storage, and required PPE.

Room turnover lab

Perform hand hygiene as though you're moving from one client to the next.
Strip used linens without shaking or unnecessarily contacting them against your clothing.
Identify all client-contact and high-touch surfaces that require attention.
Read the label on a classroom disinfectant and find its contact-time instructions.
Reset the room with clean linens and supplies without recontaminating prepared surfaces.

MBLEx check

Hand hygiene, disposable gloves, and correct handling of contaminated linens are collectively examples of what?

Standard precautions.

When might gloves be appropriate?

When contact with blood, certain body fluids, broken skin, or another exposure hazard is reasonably anticipated.

Why isn't wiping a disinfectant off immediately necessarily enough?

Disinfectants require the labeled wet contact time to work as intended.

Why are precautions used routinely?

Because you can't reliably identify every infectious risk by looking at a client or waiting for a known diagnosis.
