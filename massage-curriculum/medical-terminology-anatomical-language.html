Medical Terminology & Anatomical Language

Decode unfamiliar words instead of memorizing them as giant strings. This vocabulary is the language used throughout anatomy, pathology, kinesiology and documentation.

Build the word

Prefix comes before the root and modifies meaning. Root/combining form carries the central meaning. Suffix comes at the end and often identifies a condition, process or procedure. A combining vowel, commonly o, helps connect parts.

Example: oste/o/arthr/itis → bone + joint + inflammation.

Anatomical position

Standard anatomical position: standing upright, head/eyes forward, feet directed forward, arms at sides with palms facing anteriorly. Directional language assumes this reference even if the client is prone or supine.

Directional terms

Anterior / posteriorfront / back

Proximal / distalnearer / farther from trunk or point of origin
Superficial / deepcloser to / farther from body surface

Planes

Sagittal: divides left/right; flexion-extension commonly occurs here. Frontal/coronal: divides anterior/posterior; abduction-adduction commonly occurs here. Transverse: divides superior/inferior; rotation commonly occurs here.

Body cavities

Dorsal cavity: cranial and vertebral/spinal cavities. Ventral cavity: thoracic plus abdominopelvic cavities. The diaphragm separates thoracic from abdominopelvic regions. The thoracic cavity includes pleural cavities and mediastinum; the abdominopelvic cavity is divided conceptually into abdominal and pelvic regions.

Abdominopelvic quadrants

RUQ: much of liver/gallbladder region. LUQ: stomach/spleen region. RLQ: cecum/appendix region. LLQ: descending/sigmoid colon region. Organs can cross quadrants, so these are clinical mapping conventions, not sealed boxes.

Position words used in massage

Prone: face down. Supine: face up. Side-lying: on one side. Fowler/semi-Fowler: upper body elevated. Position choice can be therapeutic and safety-related, especially with pregnancy, reflux, respiratory difficulty or mobility limitations.

Decode these

Bradycardia

brady = slow; cardia = heart → unusually slow heart rate.

Neuropathy

neur = nerve; pathy = disorder/disease → disorder affecting nerves.

Dermatitis

dermat = skin; itis = inflammation → inflammation of skin.

Hypoglycemia

hypo = low; glyc = sugar/glucose; emia = blood condition → low blood glucose.

Splenomegaly

splen = spleen; megaly = enlargement → enlarged spleen.
