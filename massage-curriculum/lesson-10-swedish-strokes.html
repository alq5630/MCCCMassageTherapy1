Swedish Massage Strokes

Swedish massage, also called classic massage, uses a vocabulary of foundational techniques. Your course materials organize them as E-P-F-C-T-V.

E-P-F-C-T-V

E

Effleurage

Gliding movements that follow the contours of the body. Effleurage can introduce touch and pressure, spread lubricant, assess tissue, connect techniques, and begin or finish work on a region.

one-handedtwo-handedalternate handnerve stroke
P

Petrissage

Compression, lifting or decompression, and release of soft tissue. Think grasp, lift, compress, release while maintaining control and contact.

one-handedtwo-handedalternate handskin rolling
F

Friction

Rubbing one surface against another. Superficial friction moves over the skin, while deep friction moves the client's skin with the therapist's contact to create shear against deeper tissues.

warmingtransverse / cross-fiberchucking / parallelcircular
C

Compression

A non-gliding application of pressure. Compression may be sustained or applied rhythmically by pressing and releasing.

sustainedrhythmicone-handedtwo-handed
T

Tapotement

Rhythmic percussion. These faster techniques are commonly experienced as stimulating and require relaxed, controlled hands rather than forceful striking.

hackingcuppingclappingpoundingtappingpincement
V

Vibration

Rapid trembling or oscillating movement transferred into the client's tissues. Your study materials distinguish fine vibration from larger shaking or jostling movements.

fine vibrationjostling

Effleurage deserves extra attention

It's one of the most versatile techniques in massage. Keep the hand or forearm in comfortable contact, match the pressure to the client, and use your stance and body weight rather than forcing pressure through your wrists. The nerve stroke is a very light effleurage or feathering technique and is taught in your materials as moving down the body.

Technique lab

Demonstrate each E-P-F-C-T-V family and name it while performing it.
Perform one-handed, two-handed, and alternate-hand effleurage while maintaining continuous flow.
Practice petrissage as a clear grasp, lift, compress, release sequence.
Compare superficial friction with a controlled cross-fiber friction movement.
Practice sustained versus rhythmic compression.
Perform at least three tapotement variations with relaxed wrists.
Demonstrate fine vibration and then a larger jostling movement so your partner can feel the difference.

MBLEx check

Effleurage is also called what?

Gliding or stroking.

What three actions define petrissage in your study guide?

Lifting, squeezing/compressing, and releasing soft tissue.

Transverse friction is also called what?

Cross-fiber friction.

Name three forms of tapotement.

Examples include hacking, cupping, clapping, pounding, tapping, and pincement.

What are the two vibration categories emphasized in the study materials?

Fine vibration and jostling.
