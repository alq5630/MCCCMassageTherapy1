MBLEx Readiness

Bring the entire program together: anatomy, kinesiology, pathology, contraindications, technique, assessment, ethics, professional practice and clinical reasoning.

Study by decisions, not trivia

When practicing questions, ask: What information changes whether massage is safe? What structure performs this action? What technique fits the goal? What is outside scope? What is the most professional response? The exam often rewards the best decision rather than a memorized definition alone.

High-yield domains

Anatomy & physiology
Systems, tissues, structures and functions.
Kinesiology
Actions, planes, joints, agonists and antagonists.
Pathology
Signs, precautions, contraindications and modifications.
Benefits & effects
Reasonable physiological and psychological effects without exaggerated claims.
Assessment & treatment
Intake, observation, palpation, planning and reassessment.
Ethics & professional practice
Consent, boundaries, scope, confidentiality and business responsibilities.

Question strategy

  1. Read the final sentence first so you know what the question is asking.
  2. Identify safety or scope issues before choosing a technique.
  3. Eliminate answers that diagnose, guarantee, exceed scope, ignore consent or create unnecessary risk.
  4. Choose the answer that most directly addresses the question.
  5. Don't change an answer just because another option sounds more technical.

Final-week plan

Use mixed practice exams, then study the reasoning behind every missed question. Rotate through muscle actions, pathology/contraindication scenarios, ethics cases and anatomy systems. Spend more time on weak categories than repeatedly reviewing material you already know.
