Draping

Use linens intentionally to protect modesty, privacy, warmth, security, and professional boundaries while exposing only the area being treated.

Why we drape

Modesty & privacy
The client remains appropriately covered throughout the session.
Security
A stable drape helps the client feel protected while receiving bodywork.
Warmth
Sheets and blankets help regulate comfort when only one area is exposed.
Barrier
Linens provide a clean layer between the client's skin and treatment equipment.

Core draping rules

Expose only what you're working on. Maintain secure coverage of private areas, avoid unnecessary movement of the linens, and anchor the drape before repositioning or turning the client.
  • Use clean linens for every session.
  • Keep drapes controlled rather than lifting or “fluffing” them.
  • Secure the drape before exposing a treatment area.
  • Maintain coverage while the client turns over.
  • Remove bolsters before asking the client to reposition or get off the table.

Sheet and towel draping

Sheet draping
Typically includes a fitted or bottom sheet for the table and a separate top sheet for the client. Fold, tuck, and anchor deliberately.
Towel draping
Heavier and absorbent, making towels useful in spa and hydrotherapy settings. Multiple towels may be needed to maintain secure coverage.

When accessing the abdomen or chest area within appropriate scope and instruction, use professional draping and additional coverings as needed to maintain boundaries and client comfort.

Draping lab

Practice a secure back drape without lifting the sheet away from the body.
Practice draping one leg while maintaining pelvic and opposite-leg coverage.
Practice arm draping in both prone and supine positions.
Guide a partner through turning from prone to supine while maintaining coverage.
Ask your partner for feedback on whether the drape feels secure, warm, and predictable.

Check your understanding

What is the use of linens or towels to cover the client, table, and accessories called?

Draping.

Name four purposes of draping.

Modesty/privacy, security, warmth, a protective barrier, and professionalism are key purposes.

Why should you avoid “fluffing” the sheet?

It reduces control of the drape and can unnecessarily compromise the client's sense of coverage and security.

What should happen before a client turns over?

Secure the drape, remove obstructing bolsters as appropriate, explain what you'd like the client to do, and maintain coverage throughout the turn.
