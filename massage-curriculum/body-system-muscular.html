Muscular System

Three muscle tissues

Skeletal: striated, primarily voluntary. Cardiac: striated involuntary myocardium. Smooth: nonstriated involuntary tissue in hollow organs/vessels.

Organization

Whole muscle → fascicles → muscle fibers → myofibrils → sarcomeres → myofilaments. Epimysium surrounds whole muscle, perimysium fascicles, endomysium individual fibers.

Sarcomere

Actin thin filaments and myosin thick filaments interact during contraction. Calcium exposes binding sites; ATP is required for cross-bridge cycling and detachment.

Neuromuscular junction

A motor neuron releases acetylcholine at the NMJ, initiating muscle-fiber excitation. A motor unit is one motor neuron plus the fibers it innervates. Recruitment increases force by activating additional motor units.

Contractions

Concentric: force while shortening. Eccentric: force while lengthening. Isometric: force without meaningful change in muscle length/joint angle.

Fiber concepts

Type I fibers are relatively fatigue resistant and oxidative; Type II fibers generally produce faster/more powerful contractions with subtype differences. Real muscles contain mixtures.

Architecture & leverage

Parallel/fusiform fibers favor excursion; pennate arrangements pack more fibers into an area and favor force. Bones and joints form lever systems. Human movement often trades mechanical advantage for speed/range.

Tone & posture

Muscle tone is ongoing low-level neural activation, not simply “tight fascia.” Posture is variable and task-dependent; avoid diagnosing pain solely from visual posture.

Study method

Be able to identify the major structures, state their functions, connect the system to relevant pathology and explain how the information changes massage safety or treatment planning.
