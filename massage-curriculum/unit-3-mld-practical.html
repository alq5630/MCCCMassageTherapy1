Clinical Practical Block · MLD

Manual Lymphatic Drainage Practical

Translate lymphatic anatomy into a deliberate full-body practice sequence without turning MLD into deep massage. This modality is practiced near the medical-massage block but remains distinct from medical/deep-tissue techniques.

Practical, not theory-exam content

This lesson leads to an MLD Practical Competency. MLD isn't part of the numbered Theory Exam question banks. Your goal is to demonstrate safe screening, correct pressure, rhythm, direction, sequence and scope.

Technique standard

Use light skin-stretch, a slow rhythm, relaxed hands and repeated directional movements. Pressure should move the skin without digging into underlying muscle. Practice on healthy classmates is for learning the technique pattern, not treating diagnosed lymphedema.

Sequence map

1 · Central preparationDiaphragmatic breathing and gentle work near the venous angles/supraclavicular region.
2 · Neck & facePrepare cervical drainage, then work facial territories toward appropriate pathways.
3 · Upper trunkPrepare axillary territories and direct superficial trunk work according to watersheds.
4 · ArmsPrepare proximally, then progress by sections toward the hand while directing each section back toward proximal drainage.
5 · AbdomenUse gentle abdominal work with breathing and awareness of client comfort and contraindications.
6 · LegsPrepare inguinal/proximal territories, then progress through thigh, lower leg, ankle and foot sections.
7 · Posterior bodyRespect posterior watershed territories and direct superficial work toward appropriate node regions.
8 · Finish centrallyReturn to proximal/central work and reassess the client's response.

Partner lab checkpoints

  1. Can your partner feel skin movement without muscular compression?
  2. Is your rhythm slow and repeatable rather than rushed?
  3. Can you explain where the region you're treating drains?
  4. Did you prepare proximal pathways before progressing farther away?
  5. Are your shoulders, wrists and fingers relaxed?
  6. Can you identify a finding that would make you stop and reassess rather than continue the routine?

Don't memorize movements without anatomy

A student who knows a sequence but can't explain its direction doesn't yet understand MLD. During practice, name the node region, drainage territory and intended direction before performing each section. That turns the routine into clinical reasoning rather than choreography.
