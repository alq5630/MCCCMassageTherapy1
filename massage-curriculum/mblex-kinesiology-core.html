Muscles, Joints & Movement

Kinesiology questions become easier when you stop memorizing disconnected facts and reason from attachment, joint crossed, fiber direction and contraction type.

The OIA rule

For every major muscle, know origin, insertion and action, plus its location and the joint or joints it crosses. In your MCCC course, muscle identification and palpation are paired with kinesiology throughout the anatomy sequence, so the goal is not just naming a muscle on paper.

Muscle organization

Skeletal muscle is wrapped and subdivided by connective tissue. The whole muscle contains fascicles; fascicles contain muscle fibers; fibers contain myofibrils; myofibrils contain repeating sarcomeres built from myofilaments.

Epimysium
Connective tissue around the whole muscle.
Perimysium
Surrounds a fascicle.
Endomysium
Surrounds an individual muscle fiber.
Sarcomere
Functional contractile unit containing interacting thin and thick filaments.

Contractions

Concentric
The muscle produces force while shortening. Example: biceps brachii shortens during the lifting phase of an elbow curl.
Eccentric
The muscle produces force while lengthening, often controlling movement. Example: biceps controls the lowering phase of a curl.
Isometric
Tension develops without visible change in muscle length or joint angle.
Agonist / antagonist
The agonist is a prime mover for an action; an antagonist opposes or controls that action. Muscles can change roles depending on the movement.

Proprioceptors and reflex-related structures

Muscle spindle
Senses muscle length and rate of stretch. Rapid stretch can contribute to the stretch reflex.
Golgi tendon organ
Located near the musculotendinous junction and responds to tension/force.
Proprioception
The nervous system's awareness of body position and movement.
Clinical implication
Stretching should be controlled. Bouncing rapidly into end range can provoke protective responses rather than improve comfortable mobility.

Joint classifications

ClassificationMeaning / examples
FibrousBones joined by dense fibrous connective tissue, generally little movement. Skull sutures are a classic example.
CartilaginousBones connected by cartilage, allowing little to limited movement. Examples include pubic symphysis and intervertebral discs.
SynovialFreely movable joints with a joint cavity, articular cartilage, capsule and synovial fluid. Most limb joints are synovial.

Common synovial shapes include ball-and-socket, hinge, pivot, condyloid, saddle and plane/gliding joints.

Movement vocabulary

Flexion / extension
Usually decreases / increases the angle between body segments.
Abduction / adduction
Moves away from / toward the midline.
Internal / external rotation
Rotates a segment toward / away from the body's midline.
Pronation / supination
Forearm movements that turn the palm posterior/down vs anterior/up in anatomical reference.
Dorsiflexion / plantarflexion
Ankle movement bringing the dorsum of the foot toward the shin vs pointing the foot.
Inversion / eversion
Turns the sole medially vs laterally.
Elevation / depression
Moves a structure superiorly vs inferiorly.
Protraction / retraction
Moves a structure anteriorly/away vs posteriorly/toward a reference position, commonly used for scapula and jaw.

ROM assessment

AROM
Active range of motion is produced by the client. It provides information about willingness/ability to move, motor control and symptom behavior.
PROM
Passive range of motion is produced by an external force while the client remains relaxed. It can provide information about available joint/tissue motion and end feel.
Resisted movement
The client contracts against resistance. In massage assessment it supplies information, but it does not independently establish a medical diagnosis.
End feel
The quality of resistance perceived near the end of passive range. Interpretation belongs within appropriate training and scope.

Reason from the joint

If you forget an action, picture where the muscle crosses the joint. A muscle crossing anterior to the elbow is positioned differently from one crossing posteriorly. Fiber direction and attachment locations give clues. This doesn't replace memorization of OIA, but it makes recall more durable.

Rapid recall

Which contraction occurs when a muscle produces force while lengthening?

Eccentric contraction.

Which receptor is strongly associated with detecting muscle length/stretch?

The muscle spindle.

What is active ROM?

Movement performed by the client using their own muscular effort.

What type of synovial joint is the glenohumeral joint?

Ball-and-socket.

What does abduction mean?

Movement away from the midline.

Origin and insertion alone are not enough for practical competence. What else should a massage student know?

Location, palpation, action, joint relationships and how the muscle behaves during movement and treatment.
