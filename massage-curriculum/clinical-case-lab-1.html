Clinical Reasoning Case Lab · Set 1

For each case decide: proceed, modify, postpone, refer or emergency response. Then identify what information actually drove the decision.

Case 1 · Desk worker

A 34-year-old reports gradual upper-trapezius tension after a long workweek. No trauma, numbness, weakness, fever or medication change. Goal: relaxation and easier neck movement.

Reasoning: Proceed if intake is otherwise clear. Establish baseline comfortable ROM if useful, choose goal-directed upper-back/neck work, reassess response. Do not diagnose the cause from posture alone.

Case 2 · Calf concern

A client reports new one-sided calf swelling and tenderness after a long flight. The calf feels warmer than the other side.

Reasoning: Do not massage the leg. Possible DVT pattern warrants prompt medical evaluation. Do not attempt to 'flush' it.

Case 3 · Anticoagulant

Client is medically stable but takes an anticoagulant and says they bruise easily. They request very deep work.

Reasoning: Gather relevant history and modify pressure. Medication-related bleeding/bruising risk matters more than the client's belief that deeper is always better.

Case 4 · Neurological response

During a forceful neck stretch, the client suddenly reports tingling down the arm.

Reasoning: Stop the provoking technique immediately. Reassess symptoms. Persistent/new neurological symptoms warrant referral. Do not repeatedly reproduce the symptom.

Case 5 · Psoriasis

Client has chronic psoriasis plaques on elbows and knees. No infection signs; some plaques are cracked and sore.

Reasoning: Psoriasis is not contagious. Modify or avoid irritated/cracked areas and work elsewhere as appropriate.

Case 6 · Fever

Client arrives achy, febrile and says a respiratory illness began this morning.

Reasoning: Postpone. Systemic illness and possible transmission make routine massage inappropriate.

Case 7 · Pregnancy

Healthy pregnant client requests massage and is uncomfortable lying flat for long periods.

Reasoning: Pregnancy alone is not a pathology. Use appropriate positioning/bolstering and screen for current pregnancy-specific concerns; adapt to comfort and training.

Case 8 · Unknown lump

While working the back, you notice a firm lump the client says is new and has never been evaluated.

Reasoning: Do not diagnose or aggressively manipulate it. Describe what was noticed, avoid the area and recommend appropriate evaluation.

Case 9 · Diabetes

Client with diabetes has reduced sensation in both feet and requests very hot stones on them.

Reasoning: Do not rely on impaired sensation for thermal feedback. Avoid unsafe heat exposure; modify the plan and inspect for skin concerns within scope.

Case 10 · Boundary change

Client initially consented to upper-pectoral work but says 'actually, I don't want you working there' as you begin.

Reasoning: Stop immediately, cover the region and modify the plan. Consent is ongoing and can be withdrawn at any time.

Case 11 · Acute ankle

Client twisted ankle yesterday. It is visibly swollen and painful with weight bearing.

Reasoning: Avoid aggressive local massage/mobilization in the acute injury. Depending on severity and inability to bear weight, medical evaluation may be appropriate.

Case 12 · Dizziness after session

Client sits up quickly and becomes lightheaded. They take blood-pressure medication.

Reasoning: Protect from falling, allow a slower position change and monitor. Do not send them walking immediately while dizzy; escalating/severe symptoms require appropriate response.

Case 13 · Post-surgery

Client had hip replacement recently and wants you to stretch the hip 'as far as possible to loosen scar tissue.'

Reasoning: Do not exceed surgical precautions or healing restrictions. Clarify status/restrictions and use only appropriate techniques within training.

Case 14 · Athlete before event

Runner asks for a vigorous unfamiliar deep technique immediately before competition.

Reasoning: Consider timing and goal. Pre-event work should not create soreness/fatigue or introduce unnecessary risk; select appropriate stimulating/preparatory work rather than maximal depth.

Case 15 · Client asks for diagnosis

Client asks, 'Is this definitely a pinched nerve?' after you find a tender neck area.

Reasoning: Stay within scope: explain what you can observe and that diagnosis requires an appropriate healthcare professional. Tenderness alone does not diagnose nerve compression.
