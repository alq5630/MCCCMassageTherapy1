Reflexology Foundations

Reflexology applies pressure to specific areas of the feet or hands. Learn the traditional map, practical techniques, treatment guidelines, and the difference between reflexology theory and claims supported by modern evidence.

History & theory

Reflexology has also been called zone therapy. Eunice Ingham mapped reflex points on the feet and helped popularize modern reflexology in North America. Traditional reflexology theory describes ten vertical zones, five associated with each foot, and proposes that areas of the foot correspond to areas of the body.

In professional practice, present these maps as the framework used by reflexology rather than as proof that pressing a foot point directly treats an internal organ.

Map the foot

Neck / shoulder line
Between the toes and ball of the foot. The traditional head area lies above it.
Diaphragm line
Below the ball of the foot. Helps separate the chest region from areas below.
Waistline
Runs from the base of the fifth metatarsal toward the medial edge of the foot.
Pelvic line
Located toward the front of the heel and used to delineate the pelvic region.

Core techniques

Thumb & finger walking
Alternately bend and straighten the distal joint to create a small walking motion across a zone or treatment area while the foot remains supported.
Direct point pressure
Isolates a specific point. Pressure stays within the client's comfort and pain threshold.
Hook-in & backup
A point-work technique using alternating pressure to isolate a specific reflex point.
Relaxation “desserts”
Foot flop, ankle flop, plantar flexion, dorsiflexion, inversion, and eversion can be used before, during, and after focused work.

Treatment guidelines

Complete a health assessment and obtain consent. Support the feet on a clean drape, sanitize hands and tools, use efficient body mechanics, and respect a client's preference to keep socks on. Lubricant isn't required for traditional walking techniques. Pressure should stay within the client's pain threshold.

Contraindications & modifications

Apply the same standard precautions and relevant local or absolute contraindication reasoning used in massage. Local issues may include active skin infection, ulceration or fissures, painful corns or bunions, gout, ingrown toenails, pitting edema, or recent foot and ankle injury. Don't mechanically treat through an inflamed, infected, or injured area.

Practical sequence

Establish contact and begin with relaxation techniques. Use the landmarks to orient yourself, then work treatment areas with walking techniques and individual points with point work. The traditional sequence includes head, chest, abdominal, pelvic, reproductive, and spinal areas. The medial edge of the foot is used for the traditional spinal reflex area.

Check yourself

How many vertical zones are used in traditional reflexology?

Ten total, with five associated with each foot.

Who is commonly called the mother of modern reflexology?

Eunice Ingham.

What are the two main reflexology techniques emphasized in this lesson?

Thumb/finger walking and point work.

Should point work exceed the client's pain threshold?

No. Pressure stays within the client's tolerable range.

How should you talk about reflex points professionally?

Teach the traditional reflexology map accurately while avoiding unsupported claims that pressure on a foot point diagnoses or directly treats a specific internal organ.
