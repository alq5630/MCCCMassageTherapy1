Shiatsu Practicum

Practice comfortable, sustained pressure and whole-body rhythm while understanding shiatsu as a Japanese bodywork tradition with multiple schools and lineages.

Set-up

Shiatsu is commonly performed through clothing on a mat or other supportive surface, though professional styles vary. Keep the client warm, position joints comfortably, and use therapist body weight rather than exhausting the thumbs.

Pressure principles

  1. Make broad, relaxed contact.
  2. Lean gradually with body weight instead of poking.
  3. Use palms, thumbs or other trained contact surfaces appropriate to the region.
  4. Maintain a steady rhythm and clear transitions.
  5. Back off for guarding, sharp pain, numbness, tingling or vascular symptoms.

Traditional theory vs observed effect

Students may learn meridian or qi concepts as part of shiatsu's traditional framework. When communicating clinically, distinguish those concepts from measurable anatomy and avoid promising that pressure corrects a specific internal-organ disease.
