Infection Control & Disease Transmission

Understand what disease is, how pathogens move from one host to another, and where a massage therapist can interrupt the chain of infection.

Language of disease

Disease
Abnormal body function involving structures or systems.
Pathology
The study of disease.
Syndrome
A cluster of signs and symptoms associated with a condition.
Diagnosis / Prognosis
Identification of a disease / prediction of its course or outcome.
Sign
Objective evidence that can be observed or measured.
Symptom
A subjective experience reported by the client.

Types of disease

Acute / Chronic
Sudden and shorter duration / gradual or long-lasting.
Local / Systemic
Limited to one area / affecting the body more broadly.
Autoimmune
The immune system attacks the body's own tissue.
Degenerative
Progressive tissue breakdown or deterioration.
Genetic / Metabolic
Related to genes or mutations / disrupted metabolism.
Infectious
Caused by a pathogen and potentially transmissible.

Know the pathogens

Viruses
Require host cells to replicate.
Bacteria
Single-celled organisms. Some are beneficial and some cause disease.
Fungi
Includes molds and yeasts, often thriving in moist environments.
Protozoa
Single-celled organisms, some of which are parasitic.
Prions
Abnormal infectious proteins associated with rare neurologic diseases.
Parasitic animals
Organisms such as lice, mites, ticks, and worms that live on or in a host.

The chain of infection

Transmission requires a sequence of conditions. Interrupting any link can stop infection from spreading.

1. ReservoirWhere the pathogen lives.
2. Portal of exitHow it leaves the reservoir.
3. TransmissionHow it travels.
4. Portal of entryHow it enters a new host.
5. Susceptible hostThe person who can become infected.

Modes of transmission

Contact
Direct person-to-person contact or indirect contact through contaminated objects, also called fomites.
Vehicle
Spread through a common source such as food, water, air, or blood.
Vector
Spread through another living organism, such as an insect or animal.
Massage-room thinking: hands, linens, face cradles, bottles, tools, door handles, and other frequently touched surfaces can all become part of a transmission pathway if hygiene practices fail.

Practice in lab

Walk through the five links in the chain of infection using a massage-room example.
Identify five possible fomites in the treatment environment.
For each fomite, explain how you'd break the chain of infection.
Practice distinguishing signs from symptoms in short client scenarios.

MBLEx check

What is an infectious agent capable of causing disease called?

A pathogen.

In disease transmission, what is the source in which an infectious agent lives called?

The reservoir.

Food, water, air, or blood carrying a pathogen is what mode of transmission?

Vehicle transmission.

What is the time between exposure to a pathogen and the onset of signs and symptoms?

The incubation period.
