Client Positioning & Bolstering

Positioning should create alignment, stability, comfort and appropriate access while also allowing the therapist to work safely. There isn't one perfect setup for every body.

Positioning principles

Recumbent means lying down or resting. The primary massage positions are prone, supine, side-lying and seated, with semi-reclining used as an important modification of supine.

Client comfort
Support the body so the client doesn't have to hold unnecessary muscular tension to remain in position.
Alignment
Aim for a neutral, sustainable position rather than forcing a textbook posture.
Therapist access
The position should allow safe access to the intended region without excessive reaching or twisting.
Individualize
Pregnancy, pain, breathing comfort, body size, mobility, recent procedures and personal preference can all change the setup.

Bolsters, pillows & cushions

Bolsters are positioning supports available in different shapes, sizes and densities. A common cylindrical bolster is roughly 6–8 inches high and about 26 inches long, but the correct support is whatever safely fits the client and purpose.

Transfer rule: remove bolsters and loose positioning equipment before the client gets on or off the table. A support that's helpful during treatment can become a fall or transfer hazard.

Prone

Prone = face down. The anterior surface faces the table and the posterior surface faces upward.

Face & neck
Use an appropriately adjusted face rest or other safe head support. Avoid sustained cervical rotation or uncomfortable flexion/extension.
Ankles
A bolster beneath the ankles can reduce prolonged plantarflexion and improve comfort.
Chest/breast comfort
Some clients may benefit from additional chest support. Use cushions or positioning modifications according to anatomy, comfort and consent rather than assuming every client needs the same setup.
Breathing
Reposition if prone makes breathing uncomfortable or creates pressure the client can't tolerate.

Supine

Supine = face up. The anterior surface faces upward and the posterior surface rests toward the table.

Head & neck
Support the cervical spine as needed so the head rests comfortably without excessive flexion or extension.
Knees
A bolster beneath the knees decreases the demand of keeping the legs fully extended and may reduce discomfort for some clients.
Low-back discomfort
A higher knee bolster may improve comfort by increasing hip and knee flexion. Don't rely on an exact bolster height if a different setup works better for the individual.
Reassess
Ask whether the position actually feels better. A technically correct bolster that increases symptoms isn't the correct bolster for that client.

Semi-reclining / Fowler position

The trunk and head are elevated while the client remains partly supine, creating a position between lying flat and sitting upright. In clinical settings this is commonly called a Fowler position.

It can be useful when lying flat is uncomfortable, including some later-pregnancy situations and some clients with breathing, reflux or mobility concerns. Pregnancy positioning should be individualized. Side-lying is also commonly used, and the goal is maternal comfort and avoidance of symptomatic supine positioning rather than blindly following one universal pillow formula.

MBLEx clue: if a question describes a client who can't comfortably tolerate flat supine positioning, think about modifying the position rather than assuming massage itself must automatically be canceled.

Side-lying

The client rests on one side, usually with the hips and knees flexed enough for comfort and stability.

Head
Use enough pillow height to keep the cervical spine comfortably aligned rather than allowing the head to drop toward the table or ceiling.
Upper arm
Support the upper extremity on a pillow in front of the chest so the shoulder can relax.
Upper leg
Support the upper leg with a pillow or bolster near the knees and lower leg. Avoid letting the leg hang forward and rotate the pelvis unnecessarily.
Stability
Arrange the supports so the client doesn't feel as though they're going to roll forward or backward.

Side-lying is particularly useful when prone or supine isn't tolerated and can provide excellent access to the lateral hip, shoulder, back and other regions.

Seated

In seated positioning, the pelvis is supported and the knees are flexed. A stable setup can use a tripod concept, traditionally described as three support points such as the feet on the floor, buttocks on the chair and hands or elbows supported on the thighs.

Massage chairs create additional support for the face, chest, arms and knees. Whatever equipment is used, the client should feel stable and the therapist should still avoid awkward reaching.

Positioning lab

Set up a partner in prone and adjust the face rest and ankle support based on feedback.
Set up supine with cervical and knee support, then change the knee height and ask how it changes lumbar comfort.
Build a semi-reclined position without creating excessive cervical flexion.
Create a stable side-lying setup with head, arm and leg support.
Set up a seated tripod position and identify all points of support.
Remove every bolster before your partner practices getting on or off the table.

MBLEx-style check

A client is lying face down. What position is this?

Prone.

A client is lying face up with the trunk elevated. What position is this?

Semi-reclining or Fowler position.

Why place a bolster under the ankles in prone?

To support comfort and reduce sustained plantarflexion or strain.

A client reports increased low-back discomfort after you place a small bolster beneath the knees. What's the best next step?

Reassess and modify the support. Positioning is based on the client's response, not on keeping a standard bolster arrangement.

What should happen to bolsters before the client transfers off the table?

Remove them so they don't interfere with a safe transfer.

Which position can be especially useful when prone and flat supine are poorly tolerated?

Side-lying is often an excellent option. Semi-reclining may also be appropriate depending on the reason.
