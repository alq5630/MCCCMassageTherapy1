Draping, Consent & Boundaries Lab

Draping has three jobs

Protect privacy, maintain warmth/comfort and define the area being treated. A technically secure drape can still be inappropriate if the client did not consent to the area or positioning.

Before uncovering

Explain the region and what you intend to do. Ask permission before changing the drape or working near sensitive areas. Give the client a meaningful opportunity to decline.

Minimum necessary exposure

Expose only the region needed for the technique and re-cover it when finished. Secure linens so movement does not accidentally expose breast/chest tissue, gluteal cleft or genitals.

Sensitive regions

Chest/pectoral, abdomen, gluteal and upper/medial thigh work requires especially clear communication, secure draping and adherence to school, employer and jurisdictional rules. Anatomical relevance does not override consent or scope.

Ongoing consent

Consent can change during the session. Hesitation, pulling away, freezing, verbal refusal or a request to stop must be respected. A signed intake form is not permanent permission.

Boundary scenarios

If a client sexualizes the session, requests inappropriate contact or repeatedly violates boundaries, clearly state the professional boundary and end the session when necessary. Document objectively and follow policy.

Lab checkoff

Practice prone back, posterior leg, supine leg, arm and pectoral-region draping with a clothed partner/model. The evaluator should score privacy, security, communication, positioning and whether the student asks before exposing/treating sensitive areas.
