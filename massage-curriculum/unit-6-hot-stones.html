Curriculum / Unit 6 / Hot Stone Massage

Hot Stone Massage Practicum

Use heated stones as massage tools while treating temperature as a safety variable you continuously control.

Before the first stone touches the client

Screen for impaired sensation or circulation, diabetes-related sensory changes, acute inflammation, fragile skin, recent injury, cardiovascular concerns, medications that affect sensation or heat tolerance, and any other reason the client may not reliably perceive excessive heat.

Practical flow

  1. Heat stones only in equipment intended for professional stone heating and monitor water temperature.
  2. Test every stone in your own hand before it reaches the client.
  3. Introduce heat gradually and ask for feedback.
  4. Keep moving stones moving. Don't let an overly hot stone sit against tissue.
  5. Use appropriate barriers for stationary placement stones.
  6. Rotate stones as they cool rather than increasing risk with excessive temperature.
  7. Clean, disinfect and dry stones according to infection-control procedures.

Technique toolbox

Gliding
Use the broad surface for smooth strokes while continuously monitoring heat and pressure.
Compression
Use the stone as an extension of the hand for controlled, broad pressure.
Focused work
The edge can provide more specific contact, but pressure and temperature must remain controlled.
Placement
Stationary stones require appropriate barriers and repeated monitoring. Placement isn't “set it and forget it.”

The stone doesn't replace palpation. You still monitor tissue response and adjust pressure.

Stop immediately for

Burning, excessive heat, pain, dizziness, nausea, unusual skin changes, numbness or any response suggesting the client isn't tolerating the application safely.

Practicum: demonstrate safe control

  1. Select stones appropriate for the body area and intended technique.
  2. Explain your screening and safety plan to your partner before beginning.
  3. Demonstrate gliding, compression and one carefully controlled focused technique.
  4. Practice switching stones without breaking the flow of the massage.
  5. Ask for specific feedback about both pressure and temperature.
  6. Finish by explaining your cleaning and disinfection procedure.

Instructor check: Safe handling, appropriate stone selection, fluid transitions, effective communication, body mechanics, temperature monitoring and infection control.
