Hot Stone Massage Practicum

Use heated stones as massage tools while treating temperature as a safety variable you continuously control.

Before the first stone touches the client

Screen for impaired sensation or circulation, diabetes-related sensory changes, acute inflammation, fragile skin, recent injury, cardiovascular concerns, medications that affect sensation or heat tolerance, and any other reason the client may not reliably perceive excessive heat.

Practical flow

  1. Heat stones only in equipment intended for professional stone heating and monitor water temperature.
  2. Test every stone in your own hand before it reaches the client.
  3. Introduce heat gradually and ask for feedback.
  4. Keep moving stones moving. Don't let an overly hot stone sit against tissue.
  5. Use appropriate barriers for stationary placement stones.
  6. Rotate stones as they cool rather than increasing risk with excessive temperature.
  7. Clean, disinfect and dry stones according to infection-control procedures.

Technique ideas

Use the broad surface of a stone for gliding strokes, the edge for carefully controlled focused work, or the stone as an extension of the hand for compression. The stone doesn't replace palpation. You still monitor tissue response and adjust pressure.

Stop immediately for

Burning, excessive heat, pain, dizziness, nausea, unusual skin changes, numbness or any response suggesting the client isn't tolerating the application safely.
