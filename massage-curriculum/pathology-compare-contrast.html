Pathology Compare & Contrast

Many exam questions become easier when you know the distinction between two similar-looking terms.

Sprain vs strain

SprainStrain
Ligament injuryMuscle or tendon injury
Often follows joint trauma/overstretchOften follows overload, overstretch or forceful contraction

Osteoarthritis vs rheumatoid arthritis

OARA
Degenerative whole-joint disease associated with cartilage/subchondral and other joint changesSystemic autoimmune inflammatory disease
Pattern often linked to load/use and affected jointsCan include systemic symptoms and inflammatory flares

Edema vs lymphedema

EdemaLymphedema
General term for excess interstitial fluid; many causesEdema specifically related to impaired lymphatic transport
Cause determines massage relevanceRequires condition-specific reasoning; routine deep massage is not MLD

Tendinopathy vs bursitis

TendinopathyBursitis
Tendon disorder, commonly load-relatedIrritation/inflammation of a bursa
Pain often linked to tendon loadingCompression of affected bursa may be provocative

Radiculopathy vs peripheral neuropathy

RadiculopathyPeripheral neuropathy
Nerve-root dysfunctionDysfunction/damage involving peripheral nerves
May follow a dermatomal/myotomal pattern depending on rootPattern depends on nerve/etiology; distal symmetric sensory loss is common in some conditions

Thrombosis vs embolism

ThrombusEmbolus
Clot formed in place within vesselMaterial traveling through circulation that can lodge elsewhere
DVT is a deep venous thrombosisA DVT can embolize to lungs and cause pulmonary embolism

Hyperthyroidism vs hypothyroidism

HyperHypo
Excess thyroid-hormone effect: may include heat intolerance, tachycardia, tremor, weight lossLow thyroid-hormone effect: may include fatigue, cold intolerance, slowed function, weight gain

Type 1 vs type 2 diabetes

Type 1Type 2
Autoimmune beta-cell destruction causing insulin deficiencyInsulin resistance with progressive beta-cell dysfunction
Requires insulinTreatment varies and may include lifestyle, oral/injectable drugs and/or insulin

Local vs systemic contraindication

LocalSystemic/postpone
One region/application is inappropriate while other massage may remain reasonableOverall client condition makes the session inappropriate or unsafe at that time
