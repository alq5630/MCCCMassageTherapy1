Respiratory System

Air pathway

Nose/nasal cavity → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.

Gas exchange

At alveoli, oxygen diffuses into pulmonary capillary blood and carbon dioxide diffuses toward alveolar air according to partial-pressure gradients.

Breathing mechanics

During quiet inspiration the diaphragm contracts and descends, increasing thoracic volume; external intercostals assist rib expansion. Quiet expiration is largely passive elastic recoil. Forced breathing recruits additional muscles.

Control

Brainstem respiratory centers regulate breathing rhythm and respond strongly to carbon dioxide/pH-related signals, with oxygen also important under specific conditions.

Massage relevance

Position clients for easy breathing. Acute respiratory distress, severe shortness of breath, cyanosis or concerning chest symptoms require medical response rather than massage. Respiratory infection may require postponement based on illness/transmission risk.

Study method

Be able to identify the major structures, state their functions, connect the system to relevant pathology and explain how the information changes massage safety or treatment planning.
