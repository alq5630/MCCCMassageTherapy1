Pennsylvania Licensure Roadmap

Graduating from massage school and becoming licensed are related but separate milestones. Use this roadmap to organize the transition, then verify current requirements with the Pennsylvania State Board of Massage Therapy when you apply.

Current Pennsylvania baseline

Education
At least 600 hours of qualifying in-class postsecondary massage education.
Exam
The MBLEx is an approved examination for initial licensure.
Child-abuse training
Initial applicants currently need 3 hours of Board-approved child-abuse recognition and reporting education.
CPR
Basic adult CPR is required. Pennsylvania states that online-only CPR isn't acceptable and requires hands-on training through an approved or substantially similar provider.

Requirements, fees and procedures can change. Treat the Board's current application checklist as the controlling source when you're ready to apply.

From student to licensee

1. Finish the education requirement.
Make sure your program completion and education verification can be documented correctly. Pennsylvania currently requires at least 600 qualifying contact hours.
2. Complete the MBLEx process.
Create and use your FSMTB examination account, follow current identification and scheduling rules, and make sure your legal identifying information is consistent across records.
3. Complete required training and CPR.
Keep your child-abuse training certificate and qualifying hands-on adult CPR documentation accessible.
4. Apply through Pennsylvania's licensing system.
Follow the current State Board checklist and submit each required item. Don't assume that sending a document to another organization automatically completes your Pennsylvania application.
5. Watch your application checklist.
Respond to deficiencies or missing-document requests and keep confirmation records.
6. Verify that the license is issued.
Graduation, passing the MBLEx and submitting an application don't by themselves mean you hold an active Pennsylvania massage therapy license.

Temporary practice isn't the same as permanent licensure

Pennsylvania currently describes a Temporary Practice Permit option for eligible recent graduates during the six-month period after completing a massage therapy program. Students interested in that route should check the Board's current eligibility and application requirements rather than assuming graduation automatically authorizes practice.

Renewal after you're licensed

Pennsylvania massage licenses expire on the Board's biennial cycle. Current Board information states that renewal generally requires 24 hours of continuing education, including specified contact-hour, ethics and child-abuse components. There are special rules for a first renewal, so read the current Board guidance for the cycle in which your initial license is issued.

Keep CPR current as required and don't wait until renewal week to reconstruct two years of documentation.

Student exit checklist

Before leaving the program, you should know where to find the State Board website and licensing portal, how to access your FSMTB account, where your CPR and mandated-reporter certificates are stored, who to contact at the school for education verification, and which documents you still need to complete.
