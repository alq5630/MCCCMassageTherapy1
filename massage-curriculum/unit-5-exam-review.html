Theory Exam #5 Review

Ethics, therapeutic relationships, boundaries, cultural competency, transference, countertransference, and professional conduct.

Know these cold

Autonomy
Client choice and informed decision-making.
Veracity
Truth-telling.
Beneficence
Acting for the client's benefit.
Nonmaleficence
Avoiding harm.
Fidelity
Keeping commitments.
Justice
Fairness and nondiscrimination.
Confidentiality
Protecting private information.
Trust
Foundation of the therapeutic relationship.

Boundary map

Physical: touch, draping, undress, positioning. Emotional: avoiding rescue and overinvolvement. Time: predictable starts, endings and policies. Financial: fees, gifts, discounts and bartering. Intellectual: not pushing personal beliefs. Digital: social media, texting and confidentiality. Location: maintaining the professional role outside the treatment setting.

Key distinctions

Empathy understands another person's experience while maintaining differentiation. Sympathy can involve emotional merging. Transference originates with the client. Countertransference originates with the therapist. A healthy professional boundary is often described as semi-permeable, flexible enough for connection without losing the professional role.

Case practice

  1. A client refuses neck work you believe would help. What principle applies and what do you do?
  2. A client cries during massage. How do you support them without becoming a counselor?
  3. A client offers an expensive gift. What boundary concerns should you consider?
  4. A client sends a social-media friend request. What confidentiality and dual-relationship issues arise?
  5. What signs suggest countertransference is affecting your judgment?
  6. What should you do when abuse reporting laws apply?

Cultural competency

Review cultural desire, awareness, knowledge, skill and encounters. Cultural competence isn't memorizing stereotypes. It requires examining your own assumptions, learning, communicating respectfully, and adapting care to the individual client.
