Theory Exam #5 Review

Ethics and the therapeutic relationship are their own assessment domain. This exam does not include trigger point therapy, myofascial release, deep-tissue techniques or manual lymphatic drainage.

Official exam boundary

Theory Exam #5 = Ethics & Therapeutic Relationship only. Study ethical principles, professional boundaries, power differential, consent, confidentiality, communication, cultural humility, transference/countertransference, dual relationships, sexual boundaries, reporting duties, scope, professional conduct and ethical decision-making.

Know these cold

Autonomy
Client choice and informed decision-making.
Veracity
Truth-telling and accurate representation.
Beneficence
Acting for the client's benefit.
Nonmaleficence
Avoiding harm.
Fidelity
Keeping professional commitments.
Justice
Fairness and nondiscrimination.
Confidentiality
Protecting private information within legal limits.
Trust
Foundation of the therapeutic relationship.

Boundary map

Physical: touch, draping, undress, positioning and sensitive areas. Emotional: avoiding rescue, dependency and overinvolvement. Time: predictable starts, endings and policies. Financial: fees, gifts, discounts and bartering. Intellectual: not imposing personal beliefs. Digital: social media, texting and confidentiality. Location/social: maintaining role clarity outside the treatment room.

Power, consent & sexual boundaries

The therapist holds professional knowledge, role authority and access to a client who may be physically and emotionally vulnerable. That power differential creates additional responsibility. Consent is ongoing and can be withdrawn at any time. Client initiation does not erase the therapist's professional duty to maintain sexual boundaries.

Key distinctions

Empathy understands another person's experience while maintaining differentiation. Transference originates with the client. Countertransference describes therapist reactions that can distort judgment. Dual relationships add another significant role to the therapist-client relationship and can increase conflicts, exploitation risk and boundary confusion.

Communication & culture

Use active listening, clarification, respectful language and collaborative decision-making. Cultural humility means ongoing self-reflection and respectful curiosity rather than memorizing stereotypes. Adapt care to the individual client's values, preferences and communication needs while staying within safety, law and scope.

Scope, confidentiality & reporting

Massage therapists observe, document, educate within scope, modify care and refer. They do not diagnose disease or prescribe treatment outside scope. Confidentiality has legal limits, including applicable mandated-reporting duties. Don't promise secrecy that the law may require you to break.

Case practice

  1. A client refuses neck work you believe would help. What principle applies and what do you do?
  2. A client cries during massage. How do you support them without becoming a counselor?
  3. A client offers an expensive gift. What boundary concerns should you consider?
  4. A client sends a social-media friend request. What confidentiality and dual-relationship issues arise?
  5. What signs suggest countertransference is affecting your judgment?
  6. A client makes repeated sexual comments after redirection. What is the therapist's responsibility?
  7. A client asks you to diagnose their numbness. How do you respond within scope?
  8. What do you do when a legal reporting duty conflicts with a client's request for secrecy?
