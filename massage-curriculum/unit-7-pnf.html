Joint Mobilization & PNF Stretching

Use passive movement and assisted stretching within massage scope, without crossing into high-velocity manipulation.

Scope first

Massage therapists may use passive range of motion, gentle mobilization and stretching when permitted by jurisdiction and training. High-velocity thrust manipulation is a different intervention and isn't part of massage therapy scope.

Stretching concepts

Static passive stretch
Therapist lengthens the target tissue and holds within a comfortable range.
Post-isometric relaxation
Target muscle contracts isometrically, relaxes, then is gently taken into a new stretch barrier.
Reciprocal inhibition
The antagonist contracts while the target muscle is encouraged to relax and lengthen.
Active / active-assisted
Client participates in producing movement, with assistance as needed.

PIR sequence

  1. Position the target muscle at a comfortable initial barrier.
  2. Ask for a gentle isometric contraction of the target muscle against resistance.
  3. Hold briefly without breath-holding or pain.
  4. Have the client relax completely.
  5. Take up slack gently to the next comfortable barrier.
  6. Repeat as appropriate, then reassess range and symptoms.

RI sequence

  1. Place the target muscle in a mild stretch.
  2. Ask the client to contract the muscle opposite the one being stretched.
  3. Provide controlled resistance while allowing the intended action.
  4. Relax and gently increase the target muscle's length.
  5. Reassess rather than forcing range.

Safety

Don't stretch through sharp pain, acute injury, unstable joints, recent surgery without clearance, neurological symptoms, fracture risk, or any end feel that suggests the technique isn't appropriate. Warm tissue and explain the movement before applying it.
