Clinical Techniques Block

The advanced hands-on techniques are taught together as a coherent clinical family. Each technique still has its own target, hand mechanics and clinical reasoning. Manual lymphatic drainage sits adjacent to this work but remains a distinct modality with a practical competency rather than a numbered theory exam.

Official curriculum organization

Medical Massage
Conditions, pathology, red flags, massage indications/contraindications, modifications, modality selection and medication considerations. Assessed on Theory Exam #4.
Manual Lymphatic Drainage
A separate clinical modality placed near Medical Massage because of its health-focused application. Learned and demonstrated practically. Not tested on a numbered Theory Exam.
Deep Tissue Techniques
A broad clinical technique family emphasizing appropriate tissue engagement, pacing, body mechanics, client feedback and safe application rather than simply “more pressure.”
Myofascial Release
A distinct approach using sustained fascial engagement, stretch, shear or traction and reassessment of tissue mobility.
Trigger Point Therapy
A distinct technique focused on sensitive points/taut bands, familiar referral patterns, controlled sustained pressure and reassessment.
Joint Mobilization & Stretching
ROM, stretching, RI/PIR and appropriate mobilization concepts integrated with clinical treatment goals and tissue state.
Sports Massage
Goal-driven massage adapted to activity, timing, tissue state and the athlete's immediate needs.

Manual Lymphatic Drainage

Students must know how to screen for safety, use appropriate light pressure and rhythm, understand direction and proximal preparation, and demonstrate the classroom sequence. MLD does not contribute questions to Theory Exams #1–#6. Competency is demonstrated through the practical check-off.

Deep tissue isn't one technique

“Deep tissue” is a broad treatment category, not a synonym for maximum pressure and not the name of one single stroke. A therapist may use slow compression, stripping, broad forearm contact, MFR, trigger point work, stretching or other focused methods depending on the tissue, goal and client. Depth is earned gradually through tissue response rather than forced from the surface.

Technique choice is always subordinate to safety. Acute inflammation, vascular concerns, altered sensation, unstable injury, medications, client tolerance and endangerment sites can all change whether deeper or more focused work is appropriate.

Myofascial Release

MFR gets its own lesson because students need to understand fascia, tissue glide, direct vs. indirect work, sustained engagement, cross-hand techniques, traction, shear, scar considerations and why MFR isn't the same thing as trigger-point compression.

Open Myofascial Release

Trigger Point Therapy

Trigger point therapy gets its own lesson covering active/latent/satellite terminology, referred-pain reasoning, palpation, pressure release, communication, reassessment, common muscle patterns, contraindications and the limits of using referral charts as diagnostic tools.

Open Trigger Point Therapy

Joint Mobilization & Stretching

This lesson develops ROM reasoning, PROM vs. AROM, safe non-thrust mobilization, static stretching, muscle spindles, Golgi tendon organs, isometric contractions and the practical difference between Post-Isometric Relaxation and Reciprocal Inhibition.

Open Joint Mobilization & Stretching

Sports Massage

Sports massage is organized around timing and purpose: pre-event, inter-event, post-event and maintenance. Students learn to adapt familiar techniques to the athlete instead of treating “sports massage” as one fixed routine.

Open Sports Massage

How the techniques fit together

Broad restriction?
Assess tissue mobility and consider MFR or another broad technique.
Discrete tender point with familiar referral?
Trigger-point reasoning may be useful after screening.
General muscular tension?
Swedish, compression, kneading or focused deep-tissue work may be sufficient.
Movement limitation?
Combine tissue work with ROM, stretching or neuromuscular techniques and reassess function.
Athlete preparing or recovering?
Let event timing and today's goal determine pace, depth and technique selection.

Assessment boundary

Theory Exam #4 assesses Medical Massage, pathology, modifications, modality selection and pharmacology. Theory Exam #5 is reserved for Ethics and the Therapeutic Relationship. MLD is practical-only. Deep-tissue and advanced clinical techniques are taught as their own block and aren't mixed into the ethics exam.
