Clinical Techniques Block

The advanced hands-on techniques are taught together as a coherent clinical family. Manual lymphatic drainage sits adjacent to this work, but remains a distinct modality with a practical competency rather than a numbered theory exam.

Official curriculum organization

Medical Massage
Conditions, pathology, red flags, massage indications/contraindications, modifications, modality selection and medication considerations. Assessed on Theory Exam #4.
Manual Lymphatic Drainage
A separate clinical modality placed near Medical Massage because of its health-focused application. Learned and demonstrated practically. Not tested on a numbered Theory Exam.
Deep Tissue Techniques
A broad clinical technique family emphasizing depth as appropriate, tissue engagement, pacing, body mechanics, client feedback and safe application.
Myofascial Release
A distinct deep-tissue/clinical approach using sustained fascial engagement and slow tissue response. It is not the same technique as trigger point therapy.
Trigger Point Therapy
A distinct clinical technique focused on identifying sensitive points, referred-pain patterns, palpation, sustained pressure and reassessment.
Joint Mobilization & Stretching
ROM, stretching, RI/PIR and appropriate mobilization concepts integrated with clinical treatment goals and tissue state.

MLD assessment rule

Students must know how to screen for safety, use appropriate light pressure and rhythm, understand direction and proximal preparation, and demonstrate the classroom sequence. MLD does not contribute questions to Theory Exams #1–#6. Competency is demonstrated through the practical check-off.

Deep tissue isn't one technique

Students should understand “deep tissue” as a broad treatment category rather than a synonym for maximum pressure. MFR and trigger point therapy are taught beside one another because they can both be used in focused clinical work, but they remain separate methods with different reasoning, contacts and goals.

Technique choice is always subordinate to safety. Acute inflammation, vascular concerns, altered sensation, unstable injury, medications, client tolerance and endangerment sites can all change whether deeper or more focused work is appropriate.

Assessment boundary

Theory Exam #4 assesses Medical Massage, pathology, modifications, modality selection and pharmacology. Theory Exam #5 is reserved for Ethics and the Therapeutic Relationship. MLD is practical-only. Deep-tissue techniques are taught as their own clinical-technique block and are not mixed into the ethics exam.
